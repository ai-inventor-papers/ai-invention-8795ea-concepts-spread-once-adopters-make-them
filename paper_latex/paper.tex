\title{Concepts spread where they stick: network signals of interdisciplinary diffusion in science}

\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
New scientific concepts differ in how broadly they spread across disciplines.
We ask which early structural signals in a concept's topic co-occurrence network
anticipate later cross field breadth (the breadth of a concept's diffusion
across distinct scientific fields) and by what routes concepts move between
fields. Using the full OpenAlex snapshot, we identify thousands of concepts by
title matching, track their adoption episodes across scientific fields, and
screen early network indicators against a popularity baseline on held out field
groups never used in selection. Seven indicators survive multiple testing
correction, and a learned ElasticNet over all 53 indicators exceeds the baseline by
$+0.059$ in Spearman correlation for predicting rarefied cross field breadth.
An early openness index computed from home field papers only has a small positive
partial association with breadth, $+0.069$ [$+0.038$, $+0.100$] pooled over six
nonselection bodies, but adds no forecasting gain; community count indicators
lose their signal when computed from home field papers only.
The retained frontier hypothesis, that concepts next enter fields related to the
ones currently retaining them, is only partially supported by its preregistered held out test:
the conditional logit coefficient is $d_0 = 0.322$ [$0.291$, $0.355$], with positive
point estimates in all four domain groups, but a volume matched contrast is null and the
coefficient reverses under an alternative field proximity, so retention cannot be
separated from volume. As an association, this extends the principle of
relatedness from economic geography to science.
A decomposition, an accounting identity rather than a causal split, attributes 73\% of the
breadth gap between integrating and localised concepts to early contact diversity,
while frontier advance contributes near zero. The openness signal behaves like
static topical nonredundancy of the early neighbourhood; a temporal partner
turnover component could not be detected with about ten papers per year.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Some scientific concepts stay within their home discipline for decades, while
others cross field boundaries within a few years. Optogenetics, originating in
neuroscience, entered genetics, psychiatry and bioengineering; deep learning,
rooted in computer science, now appears in medicine, materials science and
linguistics. Understanding what distinguishes broadly diffusing concepts from
locally absorbed ones matters for science policy, research evaluation and the
design of interdisciplinary programmes \citep{Cheng2023, Hofstra2019}. If early
structural signals predict later cross field integration, funders and
institutions could identify concepts with high diffusion potential before that
diffusion is observable in publication counts alone.

The problem is hard because simple popularity metrics, including publication
volume, growth rate and off home publication share, already explain much of the
variance in later breadth (in our data the baseline alone reaches Spearman
0.65--0.86 with later breadth). Any network indicator must add
information beyond these baselines. Prior work has proposed several candidate
signals: community diversity of early adopters \citep{Weng2013}, collaboration
density among parent topics before emergence \citep{Salatino2017}, and structural variation in
citation networks \citep{Chen2012}. These studies typically operate on
curated concept sets without held out validation, mix units of analysis defined
at the level of individual concepts with those at the field level, or do not
control for volume based predictors.
No prior study has screened a broad set of temporal knowledge network indicators
against a shared baseline on held out field groups for the specific outcome of
size adjusted interdisciplinary breadth.

A second open question concerns the routes by which concepts move between
fields. The principle of relatedness, established for regional economic
diversification, predicts that actors diversify into activities related to their
existing portfolio. For scientific fields adopting new concepts, this principle
has been tested for entry but not for the role of currently retaining fields,
those that have adopted a concept and kept publishing on it. Whether concepts
spread next to fields related to the ones currently retaining them, beyond
relatedness to the home field alone, is untested.

This paper addresses both questions with a large scale empirical study. We
identify 12,499 concepts from the OpenAlex bulk snapshot (476 million works),
compute 53 early network indicators across seven families, and test them on
held out field groups and a confirmatory onset cohort. We build a
conditional logit model, a discrete choice model that estimates the probability
of entry into each candidate field within a stratified choice set, to test
whether retained field
relatedness predicts the next field a concept enters. We decompose the breadth
gap between integrating and localised concepts into early contact, frontier
advance and retention channels.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We screen 53 early co-occurrence network indicators against a
    five feature popularity baseline and confirm seven on held out field groups
    for predicting rarefied cross field breadth, with an ElasticNet over all
    53 indicators exceeding the baseline by $+0.059$ [$+0.046$, $+0.073$] in Spearman
    correlation.
  \item We test, in a preregistered test on 3,162 held out concepts, whether
    concepts spread next to fields related to the ones currently retaining them.
    The association holds (conditional logit $d_0 = 0.322$ [$0.291$, $0.355$]),
    but the preregistered verdict is partial: a volume matched contrast is null,
    so retention is not separated from volume.
  \item We decompose the breadth gap between widely and narrowly spreading
    concepts: 73\% is early contact diversity, near 0\% is frontier advance,
    and 25\% is differential retention (an accounting identity, not a causal
    decomposition).
  \item We show that early neighbourhood consolidation, measured by ideational
    consistency, predicts volume growth but narrower cross field reach, a
    reversal that holds in all five domain groups of the selection data and on
    the 2015--2017 cohort but is not confirmed on vocabulary free phrases.
  \item We partially replicate the home field openness signal on a fresh 2015--2017 cohort (partial
    Spearman $+0.091$ [$+0.013$, $+0.171$], with intervals including zero at the
    strictest control rungs) and on vocabulary free newborn
    phrases (frozen verdict partial).
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related}
surveys related work. Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the data,
indicators and models. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents results.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the findings, and
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states limitations.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Concept diffusion in science.}
\citet{Cheng2023} tracked nearly 60,000 new ideas in the Web of Science
and found that ideas become core concepts when they reach expansive networks of
unrelated authors, achieve consistent intellectual usage, are associated with
prominent ideas and fit extant research traditions.
Their ideational consistency measure, the cosine similarity of vectors recording which topics a concept's neighbours
use across consecutive years, predicted next year volume.
\citet{Rotolo2015} defined emerging technologies through five attributes
including novelty and fast growth but did not test cross field spread.
\citet{Salatino2017} showed that the density of interaction among existing
topics rises before a new topic emerges. These studies provide the starting point for our work but do not
screen a broad indicator set against a shared baseline with held out validation
for size adjusted cross field breadth.

\paragraph{Topic co-occurrence and citation networks.}
\citet{Callon1991} introduced co-word analysis as a tool for mapping the
structure of research fields. \citet{Chavalarias2013} found that dense term
clusters survive longer, and that density rises during emergence and falls
before decline. \citet{Salatino2018} proposed the AUGUR system for detecting
topic emergence from co-occurrence backbone dynamics. Our work differs in that
we test co-occurrence indicators as predictors of a specific outcome, cross field
breadth, rather than using them descriptively.

\paragraph{Novelty, recombination and impact.}
\citet{Uzzi2013} found that high impact papers combine conventional and atypical
journal pairings. \citet{Hofstra2019} documented the diversity--innovation
paradox: researchers from underrepresented groups produce more novel work but
receive fewer citations. \citet{Tria2013} modelled the dynamics of correlated
novelties, showing that novelty begets novelty through the adjacent possible.
\citet{Burt2004} showed that structural holes, brokerage positions spanning
disconnected groups, generate good ideas. These studies operate at the paper or
individual level; ours operates at the concept level and asks about cross field
breadth rather than citation impact.

\paragraph{The principle of relatedness.}
\citet{Hidalgo2007} showed that a country's position in the product space
predicts which products it diversifies into. \citet{Neffke2011} extended this to
regional industry diversification. Our contribution is to test whether
relatedness to the set of fields currently retaining a concept predicts entry,
beyond relatedness to the home field and conventional density measures.

\paragraph{Community structure and virality.}
\citet{Weng2013} showed that early community diversity predicts virality of
online content, with about seven times the precision of random guessing. \citet{Ugander2012} found that structural
diversity drives social contagion on Facebook. \citet{Centola2010} demonstrated
experimentally that behaviour spreads further in clustered networks, where
adopters receive reinforcing signals from several contacts. \citet{Palla2007} tracked the evolution of overlapping communities
in large networks. Our indicator screen tests the scholarly analogue of these
mechanisms: whether a concept's early co-occurrence partners spanning multiple
communities predicts later cross field breadth.

\section{Data and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Concept identification and panel construction}

We use the OpenAlex bulk snapshot (476,196,327 works; 129.4 million base works
1995--2022). Concepts are identified by Aho--Corasick title matching of 56,643
legacy OpenAlex concepts (levels 2--5) plus Wikidata aliases, with stemmed
verification. A per concept precision gate drops concepts with precision below
0.80 (benchmark precision 0.947, recall 0.659, assessed on a benchmark of 390 pairs
with 60 hand checked pairs at 90\% agreement).

A concept's onset year $t_0$ is the first year it reaches 20 grounded
publications. Home fields are the fields holding at least 40\% of the first 30
grounded papers. The frame comprises 12,499 concepts and 27,393 adoption episodes (one per
concept--field pair).

The panel is split by home field family into a development set (DEV: Computer
Science, Engineering, Biochemistry/Genetics/Medicine; 4,771 concepts), four
held out field groups (Physical Sciences 742, Life and Environment 1,113, Social
Sciences 1,352, Mathematics and Decision Sciences 165), and a 2010--2014 onset
cohort (4,356 concepts, all fields). The specification was hash sealed on DEV
before held out scoring.

\subsection{Field backbone}

The interfield backbone is a weighted graph over 26 fields, with edge weights
given by positive pointwise mutual information (PMI) of topics appearing jointly
in the same work within a preonset window (1998--2002). This backbone is used throughout for relatedness
density, gateway centrality and community detection. Its time varying versions
(computed in five year slices) correlate at Spearman 0.92 with the frozen version.

\subsection{Outcome: rarefied cross field breadth}

The primary outcome is rarefied cross field breadth $O_{2r}$ ($m = 50$): the
expected number of distinct venue fields among a fixed size random draw of 50
papers from a concept's publications in years $t_0 + 6$ to $t_0 + 8$, computed
by exact hypergeometric rarefaction. Rarefaction separates size adjusted breadth
from sheer volume. Secondary outcomes include sustained uptake, transience, and
field- and year normalised citation growth.

\subsection{Baseline}

The five feature popularity baseline is: log publication volume at
$t_0 + 2$, publication growth rate, off home share, Shannon entropy of the field
distribution, and the number of fields reached, all in the $t_0$ to $t_0 + 2$ window. This baseline alone reaches Spearman correlations of 0.65--0.86 with
$O_{2r}$ across domain groups, setting a high bar for incremental network
indicators.

\subsection{Indicator families}

We compute 53 indicators across seven families in the $t_0$ to $t_0 + 2$
window:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Popularity (E):} publication growth, author growth, author count.
  \item \textbf{Disciplinary composition (F):} Shannon entropy of field
    distribution, home field relatedness, Rao--Stirling diversity.
  \item \textbf{Landing position (G):} gateway centrality of home and early
    off home fields.
  \item \textbf{Retained frontier (FR):} relatedness density to fields with
    revealed comparative advantage (RCA) $> 1$, volume weighted density, contact
    reach (count of off home fields entered), retention ratio (share of contacted
    fields that retain the concept).
  \item \textbf{Co-occurrence ego network (A):} 27 indicators from the
    concept's ego network on the co-occurrence backbone, including neighbourhood
    novelty (share of new partners), edge persistence (share of retained
    partners), number of Leiden communities among neighbours, ego density,
    participation coefficient, brokerage.
  \item \textbf{Author overlap ties (S):} shared authorship between concepts.
  \item \textbf{External recognition:} whether the concept appears in domain
    taxonomies or curated lists.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Indicator selection and validation}

Indicators are screened on DEV by partial Spearman correlation with $O_{2r}$
given the popularity baseline, using leaving one home group out cross validation
with 2,000 bootstrap resamples of concepts. The top 10 frozen indicators per outcome
are evaluated on held out groups with DerSimonian--Laird random effects pooling
across four domain groups and Holm--Bonferroni correction for multiplicity (a
stepdown procedure controlling the familywise error rate).

\subsection{Retained frontier model}

For the field entry analysis, we construct risk sets indexed by concept, target
field and year: at each year after onset, every off home field that the concept
has not yet entered is at risk. Entry is defined as the concept reaching 5 grounded
publications in the field. The conditional logit (Breslow partial likelihood) is
stratified by each combination of concept and year, with standardised covariates
added in four nested
rungs: the baseline includes relatedness to home, log field size, entered field
density based on revealed comparative advantage and own field gateway centrality;
the second rung adds density of fields with revealed comparative advantage above
unity; the third adds volume weighted density; and the full model adds
retained field relatedness $d_0$, defined as the mean backbone relatedness
between the target field and the set of off home fields currently retaining the
concept. The specification is frozen on DEV and scored once on the held out
frame.

\subsection{Breadth decomposition}

We decompose the gap in retained off home breadth at $t_0 + 8$ between the top
and bottom terciles of size residualised breadth $O_{2r}$ (residualised on
volume) into three multiplicative factors: early contact
$E_2$ (fields entered by $t_0 + 2$), frontier advance $M$ (ratio of fields
entered by $t_0 + 8$ to those entered by $t_0 + 2$), and retention ratio
$\rho$ (share of entered fields retained at $t_0 + 8$). The decomposition is
volume stratified. Because retained breadth and $O_{2r}$ are computed from the
same papers, the shares are an accounting identity, not causal effects.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Which indicators predict cross field breadth?}

Seven of the 53 screened indicators survive held out validation with
Holm--Bonferroni correction for predicting rarefied cross field breadth ($O_{2r}$, $m = 50$),
all positive in six of six domain groups and cohort bodies
(Table~\ref{tab:confirmed}).

The first two indicators, cumulative relatedness density and cumulative field
presence, use the cumulative field history up to $t_0 + 2$ and partly reflect a
preonset footprint. After removing the preonset component, both attenuate to
about half their raw value on the same sample (field presence $+0.176$ vs
$+0.317$; relatedness density $+0.187$ vs $+0.374$), and postonset field presence
is nearly rank identical to the baseline's reach component (Spearman 0.96--1.00).
The remaining indicators are measured in the $t_0$ to $t_0 + 2$ window, but the
community count is partly the outcome measured early: it is built from all of a
concept's papers, including off home ones, and computed from home field papers
only it is null on the fresh cohort (Section~\ref{sec:openness}).

A learned ElasticNet over all 53 indicators exceeds the popularity
baseline by $+0.059$ [$+0.046$, $+0.073$] in Spearman correlation on the pooled
held out set. Held out increments by domain group are: Physical Sciences
$+0.050$, Life and
Environment $+0.059$, Social Sciences $+0.062$, Mathematics and Decision
Sciences $+0.037$.

For sustained uptake, only the number of early authors is confirmed. For
citation growth, home field relatedness (negative) and author growth are
confirmed, and the learned model reaches Spearman 0.188 against the popularity baseline's 0.015.
No indicator or learned model predicts external recognition beyond the baseline
and onset year.

\subsection{Openness composite and confirmation}
\label{sec:openness}

Six ego network indicators (neighbourhood novelty, new edge rate, community
count and participation, and, with negative sign, ego density and edge
persistence) compose into an openness index (OPEN), defined as the mean of the
six signed $z$-scored components with constants frozen on the full frame of all
12,499 concepts.

On a fresh 2015--2017 onset cohort of 1,443 concepts never used in any selection
step, OPEN measured within the home field (OPEN\textsubscript{home}) shows a
partial Spearman correlation with $O_{2r}$ of $+0.091$ [$+0.013$, $+0.171$]
($n = 573$) at control rung R2 (controlling for the popularity baseline, onset
year, contact reach and concept type), and
$+0.080$ [$+0.001$, $+0.162$] at rung R3 (adding the
preonset footprint). The confidence interval includes zero at higher rungs, and
the DerSimonian--Laird pool across groups is $+0.083$ [$-0.007$, $+0.173$]. The
signal carries no forecasting gain over the popularity baseline ($+0.002$ Spearman). The
preregistered verdict is confirmed but marginal.

The home only build matters. Computed from all of a concept's papers, the same
index gives $+0.174$ on the cohort, and the paired difference between the
all papers and home only builds is $+0.093$ [$+0.016$, $+0.169$]: part of the
all papers signal is the off home spread itself, measured early. Among the
home only components, community count ($+0.002$) and participation ($+0.050$)
are null on the cohort, while neighbourhood novelty ($+0.134$ [$+0.049$, $+0.215$])
and low edge persistence ($-0.112$ [$-0.199$, $-0.023$]) carry the signal.

An evidence synthesis across six nonselection bodies (four held out domain
groups plus the 2010--2014 and 2015--2017 cohorts) gives a DerSimonian--Laird
pooled OPEN\textsubscript{home} partial Spearman of $+0.069$ [$+0.038$,
$+0.100$] with $I^2 = 0$, all six bodies positive. The selection body (DEV)
estimate of $+0.109$ shows shrinkage of $1.58\times$ relative to the
nonselection pool.

The pooled partial Spearman of the neighbourhood novelty and churn composite
across five nonselection bodies is $+0.105$
[$+0.069$, $+0.140$] with $I^2 = 0$. On the vocabulary free frame
(Section~\ref{sec:framen}) novelty carries the signal and edge persistence is null.
In an exploratory analysis on the pooled selection frame, controlling for the
share of early home papers that are bridging papers (papers that also carry off
home topics) halves the novelty and churn association (from $+0.118$ to
$+0.056$), so much of the home signal travels through such mixed papers. Figure~\ref{fig:evidence_synthesis} summarises the evidence
across all evaluation bodies.

\subsection{Confound analysis: topical dispersion, not temporal churn}

The openness signal could be an artefact of small yearly samples. We test this
with four null models:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Fixed-$n$ rarefaction null} (drawing $n = 10$ partners per
    year): neighbourhood novelty and churn retains 68\% of the raw effect
    (pooled $+0.078$ vs $+0.116$).
  \item \textbf{Temporal permutation null} (randomly reordering the year labels
    within each concept's own time series, 200 permutations): the temporal excess is $+0.008$
    with split half reliability below 0.05, so the measured contribution of the
    temporal ordering of partner arrivals is near zero but poorly measured.
  \item \textbf{Configuration model null} (rewiring the ego network while
    preserving each node's degree, following the Molloy--Reed configuration
    model): ego density normalised against the null ($z$-scored) still predicts
    breadth (partial Spearman $-0.091$).
  \item \textbf{Split half reliability}: neighbourhood novelty and churn
    raw $= 0.48$, OPEN\textsubscript{home} $= 0.49$, rising to 0.58 after
    configuration model cleaning.
\end{itemize}

Raw edge persistence is 66\% explained by its own temporal permutation null mean
(computed by reordering year labels within each concept's time series), and that null mean predicts the
outcome at least as strongly as raw
persistence itself. The measurable signal is a static topical dispersion property of the
home topic mix. The temporal excess variants are too unreliable (split half
0.01--0.05) and failed a planted churn check, so a temporal partner turnover
component cannot be ruled out at about ten papers per year; it can only be said
not to be detected.

\subsection{Reconciling consistency and breadth}

\citet{Cheng2023} reported that ideational consistency (cosine of a concept's
topic usage vector across consecutive years) predicts next year volume with
a 53\% increase per standard deviation. We reproduce this almost exactly: our
twin negative binomial model gives $+53.5\%$ per SD. Adding log current volume
reduces this to $+1.3\%$ [$+0.5\%$, $+2.1\%$]: the raw association is
size dominated.

Net of size, consistency's partial Spearman with rarefied cross field breadth is
$-0.069$ [$-0.093$, $-0.047$], negative in all five domain groups. On the
2015--2017 cohort, the reversal replicates at $-0.111$ [$-0.197$, $-0.030$].
These bodies are selection data (outcomes read earlier); on the vocabulary free
frame (Section~\ref{sec:framen}), the confirmatory test, the reversal is not confirmed:
$-0.064$ [$-0.159$, $+0.031$].
Panel analysis tracking each concept over time shows that years of high
consistency are followed by slightly more off home entries ($b = +0.025$
[$+0.004$, $+0.048$]), so the reach penalty is a trait observed across concepts, not a mechanism operating within a single
concept over time.

Consistency and breadth are not opposed within a single concept's trajectory, but
concepts whose neighbourhood is consistently structured tend to grow in volume
rather than spreading across fields. This complements \citet{Cheng2023} by
separating the volume and breadth outcomes.

\subsection{How do concepts diffuse across fields?}

\subsubsection{Retained frontier entry}

The conditional logit model tests whether a concept's next field of entry is
predicted by the target field's relatedness to the off home fields that
currently retain the concept, beyond relatedness to the home field, field size,
entered field density and own gateway centrality.

Rebuilt on the held out frame of an earlier experiment (369 concepts, 961
informative strata), the model reproduces that experiment's result exactly:
likelihood ratio 68.6, $d_0 = 0.281$. On the development set of the independent
frame (4,486 concepts), $d_0 = 0.246$ [$0.222$, $0.271$].

On the independent held out frame (3,162 concepts, 6,978 entry events,
hash frozen specification), the retained frontier coefficient is $d_0 = 0.322$
[$0.291$, $0.355$] (concept bootstrap, 1,000 resamples; likelihood ratio
325.8 for the retained frontier term over the volume density rung,
$p < 10^{-72}$). The point estimate is positive in all four
held out domain groups: Physical Sciences 0.148, Life and Environment 0.401,
Social Sciences 0.297, Mathematics and Decision Sciences 0.065
(underpowered and outside the preregistered sign rule). The DerSimonian--Laird pool over four groups gives 0.243
[$0.118$, $0.368$] with $I^2 = 0.92$, reflecting genuine heterogeneity across
domains. Three permutation tests confirm robustness: shuffling which fields
retain the concept ($p = 0.001$), rewiring the field backbone ($p = 0.004$),
and permuting field labels across backbone nodes ($p = 0.003$).

The predeclared volume matched contrast, comparing entry rates of retained
versus fields that were entered but not retained in the same volume cell, is null:
$-0.028$ [$-0.105$, $+0.046$]. The frozen verdict is therefore partial:
persistence is confounded with volume. Under a minimum conditional probability
proximity \citep{Hidalgo2007}, the retained frontier coefficient reverses to
$-0.021$ ($p = 0.012$), indicating backbone dependence.

\subsubsection{Gateway centrality does not predict retention}

A separate preregistered test asks whether the adopting field's eigenvector
centrality on the backbone predicts retention. On 8,515 held out episodes (3,085 concepts), the
gateway centrality increment is effectively zero: $\Delta$-AUC $-0.00001$
[$-0.0006$, $+0.0003$]. The minimum detectable effect is 0.004. Gateway alone
has AUC 0.605 on DEV versus 0.506 on held out: the DEV signal is
domain specific. Verdict: disconfirmed.

\subsubsection{Breadth decomposition}

We decompose the gap in retained off home breadth between the top and bottom
terciles of size residualised $O_{2r}$ into log $E_2$ (early contact), log $M$ (frontier advance)
and log $\rho$ (retention). Volume stratified, on the held out pool excluding
Medicine (to avoid a domain that dominates the localised class):

\begin{itemize}
  \item Early contact share: 73.3\%
  \item Frontier advance share: 1.3\%
  \item Retention share: 25.4\%
  \item Difference (exploration minus retention): 0.492 [$0.403$, $0.575$]
\end{itemize}

The DerSimonian--Laird pool of the exploration minus retention difference across three
held out groups is 0.504 [$0.329$, $0.679$] with $I^2 = 0.76$.

Breadth differences between concepts are set by how many fields a concept
contacts early, not by how many more it enters later. Frontier advance, the
ratio of fields at $t_0 + 8$ to those at $t_0 + 2$, contributes near zero
because both broad and narrow concepts gain new fields at similar rates relative
to their base (Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition}). The shares describe the
outcome's composition; they are not estimates of causal channels.

\subsubsection{Diffusion trajectories: a continuum, not types}

We tested whether concepts cluster into discrete trajectory types (localised
emergence, rapid interdisciplinary diffusion, gradual integration) using DTW
$k$-medoids and hidden Markov model (HMM) clustering on state sequences of
field presence. No trajectory typology passes a naming rule: the adjusted Rand
index between DTW $k = 4$ and HMM $S = 5$ is 0.222, and held out reclustering
gives ARI $= 0.44$. Instead, PCA reveals a continuum: the first principal
component (38.8\% of variance) is a breadth axis, and the second principal
component (10.7\%) is a keep versus lose axis. Early openness (OPEN) correlates
with the first principal component beyond the popularity baseline (held out
DerSimonian--Laird partial correlation 0.120, $I^2 = 0$) but not with the
second.

\subsection{Replication on vocabulary free concepts}
\label{sec:framen}

To test whether the openness signal depends on the legacy concept lexicon, we
constructed a separate frame of 636 newborn title noun phrases (onsets
2003--2015) absent from the 56,643 legacy concepts. On this frame,
OPEN\textsubscript{home} shows a partial Spearman with rarefied breadth
($O_{2r}$, $m = 30$) of $+0.117$ [$+0.020$, $+0.218$] at the third control
rung, and neighbourhood novelty and churn of $+0.108$ [$+0.007$, $+0.211$].
Three of four estimable domain groups are positive. The verdict is partial: the
confidence interval on OPEN\textsubscript{home} includes zero at the fifth
control rung, and the $p$-value after Holm--Bonferroni correction is 0.052.
Novelty alone carries the home signal ($+0.208$ at R3), edge persistence is null
($-0.013$), and the all papers minus home only difference ($+0.056$
[$-0.024$, $+0.131$]) is not significant on this frame.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our results address both research questions posed in the Introduction.

For the first research question, seven temporal network indicators predict
cross field breadth beyond a strong popularity baseline, and these indicators
generalise across four held out scientific domain groups. Apart from the two
indicators that partly carry a preonset footprint, the strongest are contact reach (the count of off home
fields already entered) and neighbourhood novelty (the share of new
co-occurrence partners). A learned model improves Spearman correlation by
$+0.059$ over the baseline. The practical forecasting gain is modest: the popularity baseline already
explains most of the variance, and OPEN adds only $+0.002$ Spearman on the
fresh cohort. The signal identifies which concepts are structurally positioned
for broader diffusion, rather than providing strong individual level forecasts.

The confound analysis indicates that the measurable home signal is topical
nonredundancy, a static structural property of the home topic mix; temporal
partner turnover was not detected, but the test for it was underpowered. A
concept whose early home partners introduce novel topical connections tends to
spread more broadly, and this tendency behaves like a stable trait of the concept
rather than a within-concept dynamic. Community count indicators, by contrast,
are largely the outcome measured early: their signal comes from off home papers
and disappears in the home only build.

For the second research question, the retained frontier model shows that concepts tend to enter next
the fields related to the ones currently retaining them. This extends the principle
of relatedness from economic geography \citep{Hidalgo2007, Neffke2011}, where
regions diversify into products related to their existing portfolio, to
scientific concept diffusion. The novel element is retention: it is not merely
which fields have adopted a concept that predicts the next field, but which of
those fields continue to use it. The volume matched contrast is null, however,
indicating that retention and volume are confounded and that the pure retention
signal cannot be isolated at this sample size; the coefficient also reverses
under minimum conditional probability proximity.

The breadth decomposition shows that the gap between integrating and localised
concepts is accounted for by early contact diversity (73\%) rather than by continued
frontier advance (1\%); as an accounting identity this describes, rather than
explains, the gap. This echoes \citet{Weng2013}, who found that early
community diversity predicts virality, and extends it from social media to
scientific knowledge networks.

The reconciliation with \citet{Cheng2023} reveals an outcome dependent split:
consistency predicts volume growth (size dominated) but narrower cross field
reach on the selection data and the 2015--2017 cohort, though not on the
vocabulary free frame. The two findings are compatible because volume growth and cross field
breadth are partially independent outcomes.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Several limitations bound the scope of these results.

First, concept identification relies on title matching against a legacy lexicon
that overrepresents the natural sciences and computer science. The
vocabulary free replication partially addresses this, but its sample is smaller
and the verdict is partial.

Second, the field backbone uses a fixed classification of 26 fields from OpenAlex.
A finer grained classification would produce more adoption episodes but also
more noise in field assignment. Our results are conditioned on this specific
resolution.

Third, the retained frontier claim is partial by its own preregistered rule:
the volume matched contrast is null, meaning that we cannot distinguish the
effect of retention from that of volume. A larger sample with more fine grained
volume controls would be needed to isolate pure retention.

Fourth, the openness signal's practical forecasting value is small ($+0.002$
Spearman on the fresh cohort). The indicators characterise rather than predict:
they identify structural preconditions for broad diffusion but do not yield
individually actionable forecasts.

Fifth, outcomes are measured at $t_0 + 6$ to $t_0 + 8$, a horizon of 6--8
years after concept onset. Longer horizons might reveal different dynamics, and
our results do not speak to very long term disciplinary integration.

Sixth, the analysis is observational. Early network structure is correlated with
later breadth, but we cannot establish that it causes broader diffusion. The
panel analysis tracking each concept over time suggests that the association is a
trait observed across concepts rather than a mechanism operating within a single
concept's trajectory.

Seventh, the held out outcomes were previously unsealed by two earlier
experiments in the same project, so the indicator screen held out results are
robustness checks within the same frame rather than fully independent
confirmations. The fresh 2015--2017 cohort and the vocabulary free phrase frame
are the only bodies never used in any prior step; the consistency reversal and
the confound analyses rest on selection data.

Eighth, Mathematics and Decision Sciences is underpowered in both the indicator
screen (165 concepts) and the retained frontier model ($d_0 = 0.065$, CI
includes zero), so our results may not generalise to this domain.

Ninth, several aims of the original study design are not covered. A
background adjusted citation lineage (``naturalisation'') measure was screened
early and did not beat the baseline ($\Delta\rho = -0.006$, 0 of 4 groups
positive), so it is not developed here. A preregistered within concept closure
test (does closing up at home slow later entry) was not supported. Only a small
retrospective, outcome selected atlas of AI and computer science concepts was
built, so no AI specific result is reported, and the persistence filtered
relatedness density rival was not tested. The field level homophily
decomposition of citation lineage and the venue label coverage bias measured in
early screens, and the full indicator by outcome by field matrix, are not
reported in this paper.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have shown that early structural signals in a concept's co-occurrence network
predict later cross field breadth across multiple scientific domains, that
concepts tend to enter next the fields related to the ones currently retaining
them (an association not separable from volume), and
that early contact diversity, not continued frontier advance, accounts for most
of the breadth difference between integrating and localised concepts. The
home openness signal is small, gives no forecasting gain, and behaves like
static topical nonredundancy of the home neighbourhood rather than detectable
partner turnover. These findings connect the literatures on concept
diffusion in science \citep{Cheng2023}, the principle of relatedness from
economic geography \citep{Hidalgo2007}, and community structure in social
contagion \citep{Weng2013, Ugander2012}, offering a network level account of
why some scientific concepts become broadly integrated while others remain local.
