Early co-occurrence network openness predicts cross-disciplinary concept breadth

Evidence from 12,499 scientific concepts

AI Inventor

Some scientific concepts cross field boundaries within years; others remain confined to their home discipline for decades. This study tracks 12,499 concepts across scientific fields in the OpenAlex corpus, screens 53 early network indicators against a popularity baseline, and tests seven surviving indicators on held-out field groups. The composite OPEN index, measuring topical non-redundancy in a concept's early co-occurrence neighbourhood, correlates with later cross-field breadth at a pooled partial Spearman of +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100], consistent in sign across all six non-selection test bodies (I2 = 0). Separately, concepts spread next to fields related to those currently retaining them (d0 = 0.322), though the pre-registered verdict is partial: persistence and volume remain confounded.

Contributions

Held-out indicator screen

Fifty-three early co-occurrence network indicators were screened against a five-feature popularity baseline on a development set, then tested on held-out field groups. Seven survive with Holm correction, spanning two families: retained-frontier position and co-occurrence topology. Two carry negative signs (retention ratio and ego density), meaning stable, dense neighbourhoods predict narrower reach.

The OPEN index

Six signed z-scored ego-network components compose into a single openness index measuring topical non-redundancy in a concept's early co-occurrence neighbourhood. Pooled across six non-selection bodies, the partial Spearman correlation with rarefied breadth is +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100], with I2 = 0 and all bodies positive.

Retained-frontier entry

A conditional logit on an independent frame of 11,841 concepts shows that concepts spread next to fields related to the ones currently retaining them (d0 = 0.322 [0.291, 0.355]). The pre-registered verdict is partial: the volume-matched contrast is null, and the coefficient reverses under an alternative proximity measure.

Consistency predicts volume, not breadth

Cheng et al.'s ideational consistency predicts volume growth (+53.5%/SD) but, net of the popularity baseline, predicts narrower cross-field reach (partial Spearman −0.069). The growth association is almost entirely current size, and the reach reversal holds in all five field groups and the 2015–2017 cohort (selection data) but is not confirmed on the vocabulary-free frame (−0.064, CI includes zero).

Early contact diversity drives the breadth gap

The gap between broadly spreading and locally absorbed concepts decomposes, as an accounting identity, primarily into early exploration (73% of the gap, almost all of it early contact diversity). No discrete trajectory typology is stable; diffusion paths form a continuum along a breadth axis.

Method

Data and concept identification

The study draws on the OpenAlex bulk snapshot (476 million works; 129.4 million base works from 1995 to 2022). Concepts are identified by Aho–Corasick title matching against 56,643 legacy OpenAlex concepts plus Wikidata aliases, with stemmed verification. A per-concept LLM precision gate drops concepts scoring below 0.80, yielding 12,499 grounded concepts.

Panel structure

The panel is split into a development set (DEV: 4,771 concepts from Computer Science, Engineering and Biochemistry/Genetics/Medicine homes, onset 2003–2009), four held-out domain groups (3,372 concepts from Physical Sciences, Life & Environment, Social Sciences and Math & Decision Sciences), a 2010–2014 onset cohort (4,356), and a fresh 2015–2017 cohort (1,443) from a separate scan. The total panel covers 12,499 concepts and 27,393 concept-by-field adoption episodes.

Outcome: rarefied field breadth

The primary outcome is rarefied field breadth (O2r, m = 50): the expected number of distinct venue fields in a fixed-size random draw of 50 papers from years t0+6 to t0+8, computed by exact hypergeometric rarefaction. Rarefaction separates size-adjusted breadth from sheer volume.

Indicators and baseline

Fifty-three candidate indicators span six families: co-occurrence ego-network topology (27 indicators), popularity and volume (6), disciplinary spread (3), retained frontier (7), gateway centrality (7) and co-author reach (3). All are computed over the early window (t0 to t0+2). A five-feature popularity baseline (B5) of log early volume, publication growth, non-home share, Shannon entropy and field reach provides the null comparison.

Screening and pooling

Indicators are screened by partial Spearman priority: the Pearson correlation of rank-transformed indicator and outcome residuals after partialling out the baseline. Inference uses 2,000 concept-level bootstrap resamples. Pooling across field groups uses the DerSimonian–Laird random-effects estimator with I2 heterogeneity reporting. Multiple comparisons use Holm correction.

Field-entry model

For the field-entry question, a conditional logit (Breslow partial likelihood) on concept-year risk sets tests whether relatedness to the fields currently retaining a concept, measured by positive pointwise mutual information (PMI) on the field-level backbone, predicts which field the concept enters next. A field is "retaining" a concept when it has published at least two grounded papers on it in each of the two most recent years.

Results

Seven indicators confirmed on held-out

7 / 53
Early indicators surviving held-out validation with Holm correction

Of 53 early indicators screened on the development set and tested once on four held-out field groups and two cohort splits (code), seven survive. The strongest is frontier density at period end (pooled partial Spearman +0.375 [+0.279, +0.462]), which counts how many fields already carry the concept by t0+2. Two indicators carry negative signs: early retention ratio (−0.114) and ego-network density (−0.102). Concepts with higher early field retention and denser co-occurrence neighbourhoods spread less broadly. No centrality or volume-only indicator survives.

OPEN index: early neighbourhood openness and cross-field breadth

+0.069 [+0.038, +0.100]
Pooled partial Spearman of OPENhome with rarefied breadth, six non-selection bodies, I2 = 0, all six positive

The OPEN index combines six signed z-scored ego-network components measuring how much a concept's early co-occurrence neighbourhood acquires partners from diverse communities rather than reinforcing existing ones. Its components are: new edge rate (+), number of Leiden communities reached (+), participation coefficient (+), neighbourhood novelty residual (+), ego density (−) and edge persistence (−).

Pooled across six non-selection bodies (code), the home-field OPEN index correlates with rarefied breadth at partial Spearman +0.069, with all six bodies positive. The development-set estimate (+0.109 [+0.073, +0.144]) shows 1.58× shrinkage relative to the non-selection pool. On a 2015–2017 onset cohort of 1,443 concepts never used in any prior screen (code), OPENhome gives PSP +0.091 at the concept-type control rung (R2). On that cohort the B5 baseline alone gives Spearman 0.768 with rarefied breadth, and adding OPENhome raises it only to 0.770 (+0.002).

Concepts spread to fields related to those retaining them

d0 = 0.322 [0.291, 0.355]
Log-odds of field entry per SD of retained-frontier relatedness, independent frame of 11,841 concepts

On an independent frame of 11,841 concepts (6,978 entry events in 6,076 informative strata), concepts spread next to fields related to the ones currently retaining them (code). The result is positive in three of three evaluable held-out groups: Physical Sciences 0.148, Life & Environment 0.401, Social Sciences 0.297. Math & Decision Sciences (n = 165, excluded as underpowered before the freeze) is null (0.065 [−0.110, 0.234]). The DerSimonian–Laird pooled d0 = 0.243 [0.118, 0.368], I2 = 0.92.

The pre-registered verdict is PARTIAL. The volume-matched contrast (retained versus entered-but-not-retained fields in the same volume cell) is null: −0.028 [−0.105, +0.046]. Under minimum-conditional-probability proximity instead of PMI, the held-out d0 = −0.021. Persistence is confounded with volume, and the result is backbone-specific.

Ideational consistency predicts volume, not breadth

−0.069 [−0.093, −0.047]
Partial Spearman of consistency with rarefied breadth, pooled, I2 = 0, 5/5 field groups negative

Cheng et al.'s ideational consistency, rebuilt on OpenAlex topic co-usage for 12,311 concepts (code), reproduces their volume-growth estimate: b = 0.428 (+53.5%/SD). Adding current volume removes almost all of it: +1.3% [+0.5%, +2.1%], a residual-to-raw ratio of 0.021. Net of the five-feature baseline, the partial Spearman of consistency with rarefied breadth is −0.069, negative in all five field groups and replicated on the 2015–2017 cohort (−0.111 [−0.197, −0.030]). Consistency has Spearman +0.77 with edge persistence: it is weighted persistence. Stable neighbourhoods go with growth mainly through current size and, net of size, with narrower breadth. On the vocabulary-free frame the breadth association is −0.064 [−0.159, +0.031], so the reversal is not confirmed there.

Early contact diversity accounts for most of the breadth gap

73%
Share of the breadth gap between top and bottom terciles attributed to early exploration (contact diversity plus frontier advance; unstratified decomposition)

On 3,188 DEV concepts (code), the gap between the top and bottom rarefied-breadth terciles decomposes as: early contact diversity 77.9%, frontier advance −4.6% (broad concepts gain new fields at similar rates), and retention 26.8%. The primary volume-stratified variant gives 76.0% / −4.5% / 28.5%. Excluding Medicine, exploration minus retention is 0.633 [0.537, 0.727] on DEV and 0.492 [0.403, 0.575] on the pooled held-out groups. These shares are an accounting identity for the breadth outcome, not causal effects. No discrete trajectory typology passes the naming rule; PCA reveals a continuum where PC1 (38.8% of variance) is a breadth axis.

The signal is a static property, not temporal churn

+0.008 [−0.02, +0.03]
Excess NOVCHURN after within-concept year-label permutation (raw NOVCHURN: +0.116)

Four null models were tested (code). A within-concept permutation null absorbs year-to-year churn: the excess novelty-churn measure is indistinguishable from zero. The R2 of raw persistence on the permutation-null mean is 0.66. What predicts breadth is a static property of the concept's home-topic partner mix. The temporal-excess measure has split-half reliability below 0.05, so temporal versus static dispersion cannot be resolved at these sample sizes.

A vocabulary-free replication on 636 phrase-born concepts (code) gives OPENhome PSP +0.117 [+0.020, +0.218] at control rung R3 for O2r (m = 30); the frozen verdict is PARTIAL, because the most demanding rung R5 includes zero (+0.086 [−0.009, +0.190]) and only 3 of 4 estimable groups are positive. The within-concept closure test (code) is null on the development set and opposite-signed on held-out fields (−0.079), indicating that openness is a between-concept trait, not a within-concept causal mechanism.

Limitations

  • Effect size. The OPEN–breadth association is modest. OPENhome adds +0.002 Spearman on the fresh cohort beyond the five-feature baseline, no practical forecasting gain.
  • Measurement noise. Split-half reliability of OPENhome is 0.49. The temporal-excess variants have split-half reliability below 0.05, so temporal versus static dispersion cannot be resolved at available sample sizes.
  • Vocabulary bias. The legacy OpenAlex concept vocabulary is survivor-selected. The vocabulary-free frame has only 636 concepts with pre-unseal power of 0.47.
  • Mechanical coupling. About half of the OPENall signal is mechanical coupling between the ego-network construction (which uses off-home papers) and the breadth outcome. OPENhome is free of this coupling but marginal.
  • Domain heterogeneity. In the home-only build the weakest held-out group is Physical Sciences (PSP +0.025; pooled +0.069); in the all-papers build Life & Environment is weakest (+0.071). The retained-field relatedness I2 is 0.92, indicating substantial cross-domain variation.
  • Not causal. Early ego-network openness could reflect a concept's intrinsic generality, the diversity of the research community around it, or the breadth of the problems it addresses.
  • Prior unsealing. Held-out outcomes were previously used by two earlier experiments in the same project. The indicator-screen held-out results are robustness checks within the same frame, not fully independent confirmations. The 2015–2017 cohort is the only body never used in any prior step.
