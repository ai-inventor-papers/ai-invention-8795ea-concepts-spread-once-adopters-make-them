Early co-occurrence network openness predicts cross-disciplinary concept breadth: evidence from 12,499 scientific concepts

AI Inventor

Which early structural signals in a scientific concept's topic co-occurrence network predict whether it will spread across disciplines? In a study of 12,499 concepts from the OpenAlex corpus, concepts whose early co-occurrence neighborhoods span diverse topic communities tend to reach more fields later, beyond what publication volume and popularity explain. A pooled partial Spearman correlation of +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100] across six independent validation bodies measures this openness-breadth association, with the same sign in every body and no detectable heterogeneity (I2 = 0).

Six indicators compose into an openness index that captures a concept's early tendency to acquire novel, community-spanning co-occurrence partners. Pooled over six non-selection bodies, the partial Spearman correlation with later cross-field breadth is +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100], I2 = 0, all six positive. The evidence state is lead.

Method

Data and concept identification

The study uses the OpenAlex bulk snapshot (476 million works, 1995–2022). From this corpus, 12,499 concepts are identified by Aho–Corasick title matching against 56,643 legacy OpenAlex concepts (levels 2–5) plus Wikidata aliases, filtered by an LLM precision gate (threshold 0.80). Each concept receives an onset year t0 (the first year it reaches 20 grounded publications) and one or more home fields (any field holding at least 40% of its first 30 papers). The panel spans 26 scientific fields and 27,393 concept-by-field adoption episodes.

Outcome: rarefied field breadth

The primary outcome is rarefied field breadth O2r (m = 50): the expected number of distinct venue fields among a fixed-size random draw of 50 papers from a concept's publications in years t0+6 to t0+8, computed by exact hypergeometric rarefaction. Rarefaction separates size-adjusted breadth from sheer volume.

Baseline and indicators

A five-feature popularity baseline (B5) covers log early volume, publication growth, non-home share, Shannon entropy and field reach, all computed over the first three years. The 53 candidate indicators span six families: co-occurrence ego-network topology (27 indicators), popularity and volume (6), disciplinary spread (3), retained frontier (7), gateway centrality (7), and co-author reach (3). Each is tested by partial Spearman priority: the Pearson correlation between rank-transformed indicator and outcome residuals, both residualised on the baseline. Inference uses 2,000 concept-level bootstrap resamples. Pooling uses the DerSimonian–Laird random-effects estimator with I2 heterogeneity reporting.

The OPEN index

Seven surviving indicators compose into a six-component openness index: the mean of signed z-scored values of new edge rate (+), number of Leiden communities reached (+), participation coefficient (+), neighbourhood novelty residual (+), ego density (−), and edge persistence (−). The z-score constants are frozen from the development data.

Validation design

The development set (Computer Science, Engineering, Biochemistry/Genetics/Medicine, onset 2003–2009; 4,771 concepts) is used for indicator selection. Four held-out field groups (Physical Sciences, Life & Environment, Social Sciences, Math & Decision Sciences) and a 2010–2014 onset cohort provide the first validation layer. A 2015–2017 onset cohort code (1,443 concepts), never used in any prior step, provides the confirmatory test. The specification was hash-sealed before held-out scoring.

Field-entry model

A conditional logit (Breslow partial likelihood) on concept-year risk sets tests whether relatedness to the fields currently retaining a concept predicts which field it enters next. A field is "retaining" a concept when it has published at least two grounded papers on the concept in each of the two most recent years. Relatedness is measured by positive pointwise mutual information (PMI) on the field-level backbone. The independent held-out frame covers 11,841 concepts with 6,978 entry events in 6,076 informative strata.

Results

The openness-breadth association

A descriptive meta-analysis of OPENhome over six non-selection bodies (four held-out domain groups plus the 2010–2014 and 2015–2017 cohorts) yields the headline finding:

+0.069 [+0.038, +0.100]
Partial Spearman correlation of OPENhome with rarefied cross-field breadth (O2r, m = 50), pooled over six non-selection bodies. I2 = 0, 6/6 positive. The DEV selection estimate is +0.109 [+0.073, +0.144], which is 1.58× the non-selection pool.

On the 2015–2017 confirmatory cohort, OPENhome passes the primary concept-type rung (R2: PSP = +0.091, 95% CI [+0.013, +0.171]). The association is small and behaves like a static topical-dispersion property rather than temporal turnover: a within-concept permutation null code absorbs year-to-year churn, and the temporal-excess measure has split-half reliability below 0.05.

Seven confirmed indicators

Of 53 screened indicators, seven survive held-out validation with Holm correction. They span two families: retained frontier (end-of-window field density, volume-weighted field density, contact reach, retention ratio) and co-occurrence topology (community count, neighbourhood novelty residual, ego density). No centrality or volume-only indicator survives.

7 / 53 confirmed
Indicators surviving held-out validation. Two carry negative signs: early retention ratio (−0.114, concepts with higher early field retention spread less broadly) and ego density (−0.102, denser co-occurrence neighbourhoods predict less spread).

An ElasticNet combining all indicators adds Spearman +0.059 [+0.046, +0.073] over B5 on held-out groups. The practical forecasting gain of OPENhome alone is +0.002 on the fresh cohort.

Retained-frontier field entry

On the independent held-out frame, the retained-frontier coefficient is positive and large:

d0 = 0.322 [0.291, 0.355]
Log-odds of entering a field per SD of relatedness to the off-home fields currently retaining the concept. Independent frame: 11,841 concepts, 6,978 entry events, 6,076 informative strata. Likelihood-ratio vs R2: 325.8, p < 10−70.

The result is positive in three of three evaluable held-out groups: Physical Sciences +0.148, Life & Environment +0.401, Social Sciences +0.297. Mathematics & Decision Sciences is null (+0.065 [−0.109, 0.239], 165 concepts). The DerSimonian–Laird pooled d0 is 0.243 [0.118, 0.368] with high heterogeneity (I2 = 0.92).

The pre-registered verdict is PARTIAL. The volume-matched contrast is null (−0.028 [−0.105, +0.046]). Under minimum-conditional-probability proximity instead of PMI, d0 = −0.021 (p = 0.012), while RCA density becomes strong (LR 246). Persistence is confounded with volume, and the result is backbone-specific.

The consistency-breadth reversal

Cheng et al.'s ideational consistency, rebuilt on OpenAlex topic co-usage for 12,311 concepts, predicts volume growth but not breadth. In their design (next-year volume, no current-size control), the estimate is b = 0.428 (+53.5%/SD). Adding current volume removes almost all of it: +1.3% [+0.5%, +2.1%], a residual-to-raw ratio of 0.021.

−0.069 [−0.093, −0.047]
Partial Spearman of consistency with rarefied breadth, net of B5, pooled over five field groups. I2 = 0, 5/5 groups negative. Replicates on the 2015–2017 cohort (−0.111 [−0.197, −0.030]).

The consistency measure has Spearman +0.77 with edge persistence; it is weighted persistence. A stable neighbourhood predicts growth but not breadth.

Breadth decomposition

On 3,188 DEV concepts, the breadth gap between the top and bottom O2r terciles decomposes as: early contact diversity 77.9%, frontier advance −4.6%, and retention 26.8%. On the held-out pool (variant iv), the contact share is 0.633 [0.537, 0.727]. Broadly spreading and locally absorbed concepts differ primarily in how many fields they contact early, not in how many more fields they enter later.

No discrete trajectory typology passes the naming rule. DTW k-medoids and HMM-based clustering yield an adjusted Rand index of 0.222. PCA reveals a continuum: PC1 (38.8% of variance) is a breadth axis, PC2 (10.7%) is a keep-versus-lose axis.

Confound and mechanism tests

A within-concept permutation null code shows that excess churn over 200 within-concept year-label permutations is indistinguishable from zero (pooled PSP = +0.008 [−0.02, +0.03] vs raw NOVCHURN = +0.116 [+0.09, +0.14]). The signal is carried by partners from new Leiden communities arriving through mixed-field papers. Controlling for the share of early bridging papers halves NOVCHURN from 0.118 to 0.056.

A within-concept closure test is null on DEV code (density PPML b = −0.070 [−0.180, 0.040]; OPENhome b = +0.015 [−0.038, 0.069]). On held-out fields, OPENhome is opposite-signed (−0.079 [−0.146, −0.013]). Early openness is a between-concept trait, not a within-concept mechanism.

Vocabulary-free replication on 636 phrase-born concepts code (2003–2015 onsets) gives OPENhome PSP +0.117 [+0.020, +0.218] at R3, but the R5 confidence interval includes zero (+0.086 [−0.009, +0.190]). The frozen verdict is PARTIAL.

Limitations

  • Effect size. The OPEN-breadth association is modest. OPENhome adds +0.002 [−0.003, +0.008] forecasting gain over the five-feature popularity baseline.
  • Measurement noise. Split-half reliability of OPENhome is 0.49. The temporal-excess variants have split-half reliability below 0.05, so temporal versus static dispersion cannot be resolved at available sample sizes.
  • Vocabulary bias. The legacy OpenAlex concept vocabulary is survivor-selected. The vocabulary-free Frame N is small (636 concepts) with pre-unseal power of 0.47.
  • Mechanical coupling. About half of the OPENall signal is mechanical coupling between the ego-network construction (which uses off-home papers) and the breadth outcome. OPENhome is free of this coupling but marginal.
  • Domain heterogeneity. Life & Environment shows the weakest OPEN signal (PSP +0.042 vs pooled +0.069). The retained-field relatedness I2 is 0.92.
  • Not causal. Early ego-network openness could reflect a concept's intrinsic generality, the diversity of the research community around it, or the breadth of the problems it addresses.
  • Prior unsealing. Held-out outcomes were previously unsealed by two earlier experiments in the same project, so the indicator-screen held-out results are robustness checks rather than fully independent confirmations. The 2015–2017 cohort is the only body never used in any prior step.
