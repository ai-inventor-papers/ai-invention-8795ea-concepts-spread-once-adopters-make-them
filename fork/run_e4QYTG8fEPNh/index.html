Early co-occurrence network openness predicts cross-disciplinary concept breadth: evidence from 12,499 scientific concepts

AI Inventor

Which early structural patterns predict whether a new scientific concept will cross disciplinary boundaries? This study tracked 12,499 concepts from the OpenAlex corpus across 26 scientific fields, screened 53 early co-occurrence network indicators against a popularity baseline, and identified seven that survive held-out validation. These seven compose into an openness index (OPEN) measuring how much a concept's early co-occurrence neighbourhood spans diverse topic communities. Pooled across six non-selection bodies, the partial Spearman correlation of OPEN with later cross-field breadth is +0.069: small, but positive in every body tested.

Contributions

Seven held-out validated indicators

Of 53 early co-occurrence network indicators screened against a five-feature popularity baseline, seven survive held-out validation with Holm correction. Two carry negative signs: early retention ratio and ego-network density, meaning concepts with higher field retention or denser neighbourhoods spread less broadly.

The OPEN index

A six-component composite of early co-occurrence openness, measuring how much a concept's neighbourhood acquires partners from diverse topic communities. Pooled across six non-selection bodies, the home-field OPEN index gives partial Spearman +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100] with rarefied cross-field breadth (I2 = 0, all six bodies positive).

Retained-frontier relatedness

Concepts spread next to fields related to the ones currently retaining them: d0 = +0.322 [+0.291, +0.355] on an independent frame of 11,841 concepts. The pre-registered verdict is PARTIAL because the volume-matched contrast is null and the effect is backbone-specific.

Consistency predicts volume, not breadth

Cheng et al.'s ideational consistency predicts next-year volume (+53.5%/SD) but, net of volume, predicts narrower cross-field reach. The reversal (partial Spearman -0.069, I2 = 0) holds in all five field groups. Consolidation and broad spread are partly opposed.

Breadth is early contact diversity

Early contact diversity accounts for 73% of the breadth gap between high- and low-breadth concepts. No discrete trajectory typology passes the naming rule; diffusion paths form a continuum along a breadth axis.

Method

Data and concept identification

The study draws on the OpenAlex bulk snapshot (476 million works, 129.4 million base works spanning 1995 to 2022). Concepts are identified by Aho-Corasick title matching of 56,643 legacy OpenAlex concepts (levels 2-5) plus Wikidata aliases, filtered through an LLM precision gate that drops concepts with precision below 0.80. This yields 12,499 grounded concepts, each assigned an onset year t0 (the first year reaching 20 publications) and one or more home fields.

Panel construction

The panel splits concepts into a development set (DEV: 4,771 concepts in Computer Science, Engineering, Biochemistry/Genetics/Medicine and Medicine, onsets 2003-2009), four held-out domain groups (Physical Sciences, Life & Environment, Social Sciences, Math & Decision Sciences; 3,372 concepts), a 2010-2014 onset cohort (4,356), and a fresh 2015-2017 onset cohort (1,443) used only for final confirmation. The primary outcome is rarefied field breadth O2r (m = 50): the expected number of distinct venue fields among a fixed-size random draw of papers, separating size-adjusted breadth from sheer volume.

Indicator screening and the OPEN index

Fifty-three candidate indicators span six families: ego-network topology (27), popularity (6), disciplinary spread (3), retained frontier (7), gateway centrality (7), and co-author reach (3). All are computed in the early window t0 to t0+2 and screened on DEV by partial Spearman priority against a five-feature popularity baseline (B5: log volume, growth, non-home share, Shannon entropy, field reach). Confirmation requires Holm-corrected p < 0.05 and a 95% CI excluding zero on the DerSimonian-Laird pooled estimate.

The OPEN index is the mean of six signed z-scored components from the home-field co-occurrence graph: new edge rate (+), number of Leiden communities reached (+), participation coefficient (+), neighbourhood novelty residual (+), ego density (-), and edge persistence (-). High-OPEN concepts have diverse, non-redundant early neighbourhoods.

Field-entry model

A conditional logit (Breslow partial likelihood) on concept-year risk sets tests whether concepts spread next to fields related to the ones currently retaining them. The model runs on an independent frame of 11,841 concepts (6,978 entry events in 6,076 informative strata), controlling for home relatedness, field size, entered density, gateway centrality, RCA density, and volume density. A field is "retaining" a concept when it has published at least two grounded papers on it in each of the two most recent years. Relatedness is measured by positive pointwise mutual information (PMI) on the field-level backbone.

Seven of 53 screened indicators survive held-out validation (code). They span two families: retained frontier (field density, volume-weighted density, contact reach, retention ratio) and co-occurrence topology (community count, neighbourhood novelty residual, ego density). Two carry negative signs: early retention ratio (-0.114) and ego density (-0.102).

The OPEN index was tested on a 2015-2017 onset cohort (1,443 concepts) never used in any prior screen (code). Pooled across six non-selection bodies (code), the DerSimonian-Laird estimate is +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100] with I2 = 0, all six bodies positive. The development-set estimate (+0.109) shows 1.58x shrinkage relative to the non-selection pool. The practical forecasting gain is small: OPENhome adds only +0.002 Spearman on the fresh cohort.

A vocabulary-free replication on 636 phrase-born concepts (code) gives OPENhome PSP +0.117 [+0.020, +0.218] at control rung R3; the verdict is partial (most demanding rung includes zero, Holm p = 0.052).

Concepts spread to retained-field neighbours

d0 = 0.322
[0.291, 0.355], LR = 325.8, p < 10-70
Retained-frontier coefficient (log-odds of field entry per SD of relatedness to off-home retaining fields), independent frame of 11,841 concepts

On the independent held-out frame (code), concepts enter fields that are related (by PMI on the field backbone) to the off-home fields currently retaining them. The result is positive in three of three evaluable held-out groups: Physical Sciences 0.148, Life & Environment 0.401, Social Sciences 0.297. Math & Decision Sciences (n = 165) is null (0.065 [-0.110, 0.234]). The DerSimonian-Laird pooled d0 across four held-out groups is 0.243 [0.118, 0.368] with I2 = 0.92.

The pre-registered verdict is PARTIAL. The volume-matched contrast is null: -0.028 [-0.105, +0.046]. The dose response is not monotone. Under minimum-conditional-probability proximity instead of PMI, d0 reverses to -0.021. Persistence is confounded with volume, and the result is backbone-specific.

Partial Spearman of ideational consistency with rarefied breadth, net of B5 baseline

Rebuilding Cheng et al.'s ideational consistency on OpenAlex topic co-usage (code), the model reproduces their estimate for volume growth: b = 0.428 (+53.5%/SD). Adding current volume removes almost all of it (residual-to-raw ratio 0.021). Net of the B5 baseline, consistency's partial Spearman with rarefied breadth is -0.069 [-0.093, -0.047] on the pooled bodies, replicating on the 2015-2017 cohort (-0.111 [-0.197, -0.030]). Consolidation predicts local growth; breadth requires the opposite.

Breadth is mostly early contact diversity

73%
DEV 77.9%, held-out pool 63.3% [53.7%, 72.7%]
Share of the breadth gap attributable to early contact diversity

On DEV (code), the breadth gap between the top and bottom O2r terciles decomposes as: early contact diversity 77.9%, frontier advance -4.6%, retention 26.8%. What distinguishes broadly spreading concepts is how many fields they contact early, not how many more fields they enter per year thereafter.

No discrete trajectory typology is stable: DTW k-medoids and HMM-based clustering give adjusted Rand index 0.222. PCA reveals a continuum along a breadth axis (PC1, 38.8% of variance).

Confound analysis

Four null models test whether the openness signal is a measurement artefact (code). Fixed-size rarefaction (10 papers/year) retains 68% of the association. A within-concept permutation null absorbs year-to-year churn: the temporal excess is indistinguishable from zero (pooled PSP +0.008 [-0.02, +0.03] vs raw +0.116 [+0.09, +0.14]). What predicts breadth is a static property of the concept's topic partner mix, not demonstrable temporal turnover. A within-concept closure test (code) is null on DEV and opposite-signed on held-out fields, confirming that openness operates as a between-concept trait rather than a within-concept mechanism.

Limitations

  • Effect size. The OPEN-breadth association is modest. OPENhome adds no forecasting gain over B5 on the fresh cohort (+0.002 [-0.003, +0.008]).
  • Measurement noise. Split-half reliability of OPENhome is 0.49. The temporal-excess variants have split-half reliability below 0.05, so temporal versus static dispersion cannot be resolved at available sample sizes.
  • Vocabulary bias. The legacy OpenAlex concept vocabulary is survivor-selected. The vocabulary-free frame is small (636 concepts) with pre-unseal power of 0.47.
  • Mechanical coupling. About half of the OPENall signal is mechanical coupling between the ego-network construction (which uses off-home papers) and the breadth outcome. OPENhome is free of this coupling but marginal.
  • Domain heterogeneity. Life & Environment shows the weakest OPEN signal (PSP +0.042 vs pooled +0.069). The retained-field relatedness I2 is 0.92.
  • Not causal. Early ego-network openness could reflect a concept's intrinsic generality, the diversity of the research community around it, or the breadth of the problems it addresses.
  • Prior unsealing. Held-out outcomes were previously unsealed by two earlier experiments in the same project, so the indicator-screen held-out results are robustness checks rather than fully independent confirmations.
