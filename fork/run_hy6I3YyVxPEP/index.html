Early co-occurrence network openness predicts cross-disciplinary concept breadth

Evidence from 12,499 scientific concepts

AI Inventor

Which early signals predict whether a scientific concept will cross disciplinary boundaries? We tracked 12,499 concepts from the OpenAlex corpus and found that the diversity and novelty of a concept's early topic co-occurrence partners predict later cross-field breadth. Seven indicators survive held-out validation and compose into an openness index whose pooled partial correlation with breadth is +0.069 across six independent validation groups: small, but positive in every one.

Contributions

Seven confirmed network indicators

Of 53 early co-occurrence network indicators screened against a five-feature popularity baseline, seven survive held-out validation for predicting rarefied cross-field breadth. Two carry negative signs: concepts with higher early field retention or denser ego-networks spread less broadly.

The OPEN openness index

A six-component composite measuring a concept's early tendency to acquire diverse, community-spanning co-occurrence partners. Pooled across six non-selection bodies, its partial Spearman correlation with later breadth is +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100], I2 = 0, all bodies positive.

Retained-frontier entry

Concepts spread next to fields related to those currently retaining them (d0 = +0.322 [+0.291, +0.355] on 11,841 concepts). The pre-registered verdict is PARTIAL: the volume-matched contrast is null, persistence and volume remain confounded, and the result is backbone-specific.

Consistency predicts growth, not breadth

Cheng et al.'s (2023) ideational consistency predicts volume growth but narrower cross-field reach. This reversal holds in all five field groups and replicates on a fresh cohort.

Contact diversity drives the breadth gap

Early contact diversity accounts for 73% of the breadth gap between broadly and narrowly spreading concepts. No discrete trajectory typology is stable; diffusion paths form a continuum along a breadth axis.

Method

The study starts from the OpenAlex bulk snapshot: 476 million works, of which 129.4 million are base works published between 1995 and 2022. Concepts are identified by Aho-Corasick title matching of 56,643 legacy OpenAlex concepts (levels 2–5) plus Wikidata aliases, with stemmed verification. A per-concept LLM precision gate drops concepts with precision below 0.80, yielding 12,499 grounded concepts.

These are split into a development set (DEV, 4,771 concepts with homes in Computer Science, Engineering, Biochemistry/Genetics/Medicine and Medicine, onset 2003–2009), four held-out domain groups (3,372 concepts in Physical Sciences, Life & Environment, Social Sciences and Math & Decision Sciences), a 2010–2014 onset cohort (4,356), and a fresh 2015–2017 cohort (1,443) never used in any earlier step.

The primary outcome is rarefied field breadth (O2r, m = 50): the expected number of distinct venue fields among 50 randomly drawn papers from years t0+6 to t0+8, computed by exact hypergeometric rarefaction. This separates size-adjusted breadth from sheer volume.

For each concept, 53 early indicators across six families (ego-network topology, popularity, disciplinary spread, retained frontier, gateway centrality and co-author reach) are computed over the first three years (t0 to t0+2). These are screened against a five-feature popularity baseline (B5: log early volume, publication growth, non-home share, Shannon entropy, field reach) using partial Spearman priority with 2,000 concept-level bootstrap resamples. Cross-body pooling uses the DerSimonian–Laird random-effects estimator with I2 heterogeneity reporting.

Seven indicators survive. They compose into the OPEN index, a six-component measure of early co-occurrence openness: new edge rate, number of Leiden communities reached, participation coefficient, neighbourhood novelty residual (all positive-signed), and ego density and edge persistence (both negative-signed). The z-score constants are frozen from DEV data.

Separately, a conditional logit (Breslow partial likelihood) models which field a concept enters next, stratified by concept-year. It tests whether relatedness to fields currently retaining the concept predicts entry, beyond relatedness to the home field, field size, entered density, gateway centrality, and conventional density measures. A field is "retaining" a concept when it has published at least two grounded papers on the concept in each of the two most recent years.

Results

Indicator screen and the OPEN index

+0.069 [+0.038, +0.100]

Pooled partial Spearman of OPENhome with rarefied cross-field breadth across six non-selection bodies. I2 = 0, all six positive. A single descriptive estimate. (code)

Of 53 screened indicators, seven survive held-out validation with Holm correction. (code) The strongest confirmed indicator is end-of-window field density (M0_density_end, pooled partial Spearman +0.375). Two confirmed indicators carry negative signs: early retention ratio (−0.114) and ego density (−0.102), meaning concepts with higher early field retention or denser local networks spread less broadly.

The DEV estimate is +0.109 [+0.073, +0.144], 1.58 times the non-selection pool, showing expected shrinkage from selection. On the 2015–2017 fresh cohort (1,443 concepts, never used in selection), OPENhome gives partial Spearman +0.091 [+0.013, +0.171] at control rung R2. (code)

A within-concept permutation null absorbs the year-to-year churn component (excess NOVCHURN pooled +0.008 [−0.02, +0.03]), indicating that what predicts breadth is a static property of the concept's home-topic partner mix, not temporal turnover. (code)

Retained-frontier entry

d0 = 0.322 [0.291, 0.355]

Retained-frontier coefficient from the conditional-logit entry model on the independent held-out frame: 11,841 concepts, 6,978 entry events, 6,076 informative strata. Log-odds of entering a field per SD of relatedness to the off-home fields currently retaining the concept. Likelihood-ratio test vs. the R2 model: 325.8, p < 10−70. (code)

Positive in three of three evaluable held-out groups: Physical Sciences 0.148, Life & Environment 0.401, Social Sciences 0.297. Mathematics & Decision Sciences (n = 165) is null: 0.065 [−0.109, 0.239]. DerSimonian–Laird pooled d0 = 0.243 [0.118, 0.368], I2 = 0.92.

The pre-registered verdict is PARTIAL. The volume-matched contrast (comparing entry rates of retained versus entered-but-not-retained fields in the same volume cell) is null: −0.028 [−0.105, +0.046]. Under minimum-conditional-probability proximity instead of PMI, d0 = −0.021. Persistence and volume remain confounded, and the result is backbone-specific.

Consistency predicts growth, not breadth

+53.5%/SD raw, +1.3% net of volume, −0.069 with breadth

Rebuilding Cheng et al.'s (2023) ideational consistency on OpenAlex topic co-usage for 12,311 concepts reproduces their volume estimate: b = 0.428 (+53.5%/SD for next-year volume). Adding current volume removes almost all of it: +1.3% [+0.5%, +2.1%], a residual-to-raw ratio of 0.021. (code)

Net of B5, the partial Spearman of consistency with rarefied breadth is −0.069 [−0.093, −0.047] on the pooled bodies (I2 = 0, 5/5 groups negative), replicating on the 2015–2017 cohort (−0.111 [−0.197, −0.030]). The consistency measure has Spearman +0.77 with edge persistence. A stable neighbourhood predicts growth but not breadth.

Breadth decomposition

77.9% early contact diversity (DEV), 63.3% held-out

On 3,188 DEV concepts, the breadth gap between the top and bottom O2r terciles decomposes as: early contact diversity 77.9%, retention 26.8%, frontier advance −4.6%. (code) On the held-out pool excluding Medicine, the contact share is 0.633 [0.537, 0.727]. Broadly spreading and narrowly spreading concepts differ primarily in how many fields they contact early, not in how many more fields they enter later.

No discrete trajectory typology passes the naming rule: DTW k-medoids and HMM-based clustering on state sequences give an adjusted Rand index of 0.222. PCA reveals a continuum, with PC1 (38.8% of variance) as a breadth axis. A within-concept closure test is null on DEV and opposite-signed on held-out (−0.079 [−0.146, −0.013]): openness operates as a between-concept trait, not a within-concept mechanism. (code)

Vocabulary-free replication

+0.117 [+0.020, +0.218] on 636 phrase-born concepts

To address survivorship bias in the legacy vocabulary, 636 vocabulary-free phrase-born concepts (2003–2015 onsets) were identified. (code) OPENhome gives partial Spearman +0.117 [+0.020, +0.218] at control rung R3 for O2r (m = 30). The verdict is partial: the most demanding rung's confidence interval includes zero (+0.086 [−0.009, +0.190]).

Limitations

  • Effect size. The OPEN–breadth association is modest. OPENhome adds no forecasting gain over the popularity baseline (+0.002 [−0.003, +0.008] Spearman).
  • Measurement noise. Split-half reliability of OPENhome is 0.49. The temporal-excess variants have split-half reliability below 0.05, so temporal versus static dispersion cannot be resolved at available sample sizes.
  • Vocabulary bias. The legacy OpenAlex concept vocabulary is survivor-selected. The vocabulary-free frame has only 636 concepts with pre-unseal power of 0.47.
  • Coupling. About half of the OPENall signal is mechanical coupling between the ego-network construction (which uses off-home papers) and the breadth outcome. OPENhome is free of this coupling but marginal.
  • Domain heterogeneity. Life & Environment shows the weakest OPEN signal (partial Spearman +0.042 vs pooled +0.069). The retained-field relatedness I2 is 0.92.
  • Not causal. Early ego-network openness could reflect a concept's intrinsic generality, the diversity of the research community around it, or the breadth of the problems it addresses.
  • Prior unsealing. Held-out outcomes were previously unsealed by two earlier experiments in the same project, so the indicator-screen held-out results are robustness checks, not fully independent confirmations.
