Early co-occurrence network openness is associated with cross-disciplinary concept breadth: evidence from 12,499 scientific concepts

AI Inventor

Which features of a new scientific concept's early co-occurrence network predict whether it will spread across disciplines? We tracked 12,499 concepts across 26 fields in the OpenAlex corpus and found that concepts whose early topic neighbourhoods span diverse communities tend to reach more fields later. The association is small but consistent: pooled across six non-selection bodies (five of them analysed in earlier steps, so the pool is descriptive), the partial correlation between early network openness and later cross-field breadth is +0.069, positive in every group. Separately, concepts spread next to fields related to those currently retaining them, but this effect cannot be fully separated from volume.

Contributions

  1. Indicator screening across held-out field groups

    Screened 53 early co-occurrence network indicators against a five-feature popularity baseline and confirmed 7 on held-out field groups for predicting rarefied cross-field breadth. Two of the seven carry negative signs: early retention ratio and ego-network density.

  2. The OPEN index

    A six-component composite of early co-occurrence openness measures topical non-redundancy in a concept's neighbourhood. Pooled across six non-selection bodies, the home-field OPEN index gives partial Spearman +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100], I² = 0, all bodies positive.

  3. Retained-frontier entry model

    Concepts spread next to fields related to those currently retaining them: on an independent frame of 11,841 concepts, the retained-frontier coefficient is d0 = +0.322 [+0.291, +0.355]. The pre-registered verdict is PARTIAL because the volume-matched contrast is null and the effect reverses under an alternative proximity measure.

  4. Consistency-breadth reversal

    Early neighbourhood consolidation, measured by Cheng et al.'s ideational consistency, predicts volume growth (+53.5%/SD) but narrower cross-field reach once volume is controlled. Net of the popularity baseline, its partial Spearman with breadth is −0.069, negative in all five field groups. These are selection data; on the vocabulary-free frame the association is −0.064 [−0.159, +0.031], so the reversal is not confirmed there.

  5. Breadth decomposition

    Early exploration accounts for about three-quarters of the breadth gap between integrating and localised concepts (73% in the unstratified decomposition, almost all of it early contact diversity). No discrete trajectory typology passes the naming rule; diffusion paths form a continuum.

Method

Data and concept identification

The study uses the OpenAlex bulk snapshot (476,196,327 works; 129.4 million base works, 1995–2022). Concepts are identified by Aho–Corasick title matching of 56,643 legacy OpenAlex concepts plus Wikidata aliases, with stemmed verification. A per-concept LLM precision gate drops concepts below 0.80 precision, yielding 12,499 grounded concepts.

Panel construction

A concept's onset year t0 is the first year it reaches 20 grounded publications. The panel is split into a development set (DEV: 4,771 concepts from Computer Science, Engineering, Biochemistry/Genetics/Medicine and Medicine homes, onsets 2003–2009), four held-out domain groups (Physical Sciences, Life & Environment, Social Sciences, Math & Decision Sciences; 3,372 concepts), a 2010–2014 onset cohort (4,356 concepts), and a fresh 2015–2017 cohort (1,443 concepts) from a separate scan.

Outcome

The primary outcome is rarefied field breadth O2r (m = 50): the expected number of distinct venue fields among a fixed-size random draw of 50 papers from a concept's publications in years t0+6 to t0+8, computed by exact hypergeometric rarefaction. Rarefaction separates size-adjusted breadth from sheer volume.

Baseline and 53 indicators

A five-feature popularity baseline (B5) consisting of log early volume, publication growth, non-home share, Shannon entropy and field reach is computed over the first three years. Against this baseline, 53 candidate indicators are screened across six families: ego-network topology (27 indicators), popularity (6), disciplinary spread (3), retained frontier (7), gateway and landing (7), and co-author structure (3).

The OPEN index

OPEN is the mean of six signed z-scored ego-network components from the early window: new edge rate (positive sign), number of Leiden communities reached (positive), participation coefficient (positive), neighbourhood novelty residual (positive), ego density (negative sign), and edge persistence (negative sign). Three builds are tested: OPENhome (home-field graph only), OPENall (corpus-wide), and OPENsizematch (corpus-wide subsampled to home counts).

Statistical framework

Indicator screening uses partial Spearman priority: the Pearson correlation of rank-transformed indicator and outcome residuals, both residualised on baseline covariates. Inference uses 2,000 concept-level bootstrap resamples. Pooling across field groups uses the DerSimonian–Laird random-effects estimator with I² heterogeneity reporting. Multiple comparisons use Holm correction.

Field-entry model

A conditional logit (Breslow partial likelihood) on concept-year risk sets tests whether relatedness to retaining fields predicts which field a concept enters next. The model is stratified by concept-year, with standardised covariates: home relatedness, log field size, density over entered fields, gateway centrality, RCA-based density, volume-weighted density, and retained-field relatedness d0. The specification was frozen on DEV and scored once on the independent held-out frame of 11,841 concepts (6,978 entry events in 6,076 informative strata).

Results

Indicator screen: 7 of 53 confirmed

Seven of the 53 screened indicators survive held-out validation with Holm correction. These span two families: retained frontier (end-of-window field density, volume-weighted field density, contact reach, retention ratio) and co-occurrence topology (community count, novel-partner share, ego density). Two confirmed indicators carry negative signs: the early retention ratio (−0.114, concepts with higher early field retention spread less broadly) and ego density (−0.102, denser local neighbourhoods go with narrower spread). Code: indicator screen

+0.069 Pooled partial Spearman of OPEN with rarefied breadth

The home-field OPEN index (OPENhome), pooled across six non-selection bodies (four held-out domain groups plus the 2010–2014 and 2015–2017 cohorts) by DerSimonian–Laird, gives partial Spearman +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100] with I² = 0, all six bodies positive. The development-set estimate (+0.109) shows 1.58× shrinkage relative to the non-selection pool. The association is small and has the same sign in every body, but only the two cohort rows and the pool have confidence intervals above zero individually. Code: evidence pool

The OPEN index was tested on a 2015–2017 onset cohort (1,443 concepts) never used in any prior screen. OPENhome passes the primary concept-type rung (R2: PSP = +0.091, 95% CI [+0.013, +0.171], Holm p = 0.048), but its DerSimonian–Laird pooled interval includes zero (+0.083 [−0.007, +0.173]), making the home-only signal marginal. Code: cohort test

The stronger OPENall signal is partly mechanically coupled: early off-home papers that enter the ego network are a subset of the papers that determine the outcome. OPENhome is free of this coupling but marginal. The practical forecasting gain is small: OPENhome adds only +0.002 Spearman on the fresh cohort over B5.

Consistency predicts volume, not breadth

Cheng et al.'s ideational consistency, rebuilt on OpenAlex topic co-usage for 12,311 concepts, reproduces their estimate for volume: b = 0.428 (+53.5%/SD). Adding current volume removes almost all of it: +1.3% [+0.5%, +2.1%]. Net of the popularity baseline, the partial Spearman of consistency with rarefied breadth is −0.069 [−0.093, −0.047] on the pooled bodies (I² = 0, 5/5 groups negative), replicating on the 2015–2017 cohort (−0.111). The consistency measure has Spearman +0.77 with edge persistence: it is weighted persistence. A stable neighbourhood goes with growth mainly because it goes with current size. These bodies are selection data; on the vocabulary-free frame the breadth association is −0.064 [−0.159, +0.031], so the reversal is not confirmed there. Code: consistency analysis

Field entry: retained-frontier relatedness

d0 = 0.322 Retained-frontier coefficient (held-out frame)

On an independent frame of 11,841 concepts (6,978 entry events), the retained-frontier coefficient is d0 = 0.322 [0.291, 0.355]: the log-odds of entering a field per SD of relatedness to the off-home fields that currently retain the concept. The result is positive in all three evaluable held-out groups: Physical Sciences 0.148, Life & Environment 0.401, Social Sciences 0.297. Mathematics & Decision Sciences (n = 165) is null (0.065 [−0.110, 0.234]). The DerSimonian–Laird pooled estimate is 0.243 [0.118, 0.368] with high heterogeneity (I² = 0.92). Code: entry model

Verdict: PARTIAL. The volume-matched contrast (comparing entry rates of retained versus entered-but-not-retained fields in the same volume cell) is null: −0.028 [−0.105, +0.046]. Under minimum-conditional-probability proximity instead of PMI, the held-out d0 reverses (−0.021). Persistence is confounded with volume, and the result is backbone-specific.

Breadth decomposition

On 3,188 DEV concepts, the breadth gap between the top and bottom rarefied-breadth terciles decomposes as: early contact diversity 77.9%, frontier advance −4.6%, retention 26.8% (unstratified). The primary volume-stratified variant gives 76.0% / −4.5% / 28.5%. Exploration minus retention is 0.464 [0.407, 0.528]. Early contact diversity accounts for most of the breadth gap; frontier advance contributes almost nothing. These shares are an accounting identity, not causal effects. Code: decomposition

Confound analysis

The within-concept permutation null absorbs year-to-year churn: the excess NOVCHURN pooled partial Spearman is +0.008 [−0.02, +0.03], compared to raw +0.116 [+0.09, +0.14]. What predicts breadth is a static property of the concept's home-topic partner mix, not temporal turnover. Split-half reliability of NOVCHURN is 0.48; the temporal-excess variants have reliability below 0.05, so temporal versus static dispersion cannot be resolved at available sample sizes. Code: confound tests

A within-concept closure test is null on the development set (density PPML b = −0.070 [−0.180, 0.040]; OPENhome b = +0.015 [−0.038, 0.069]). On held-out fields, OPENhome is opposite-signed (−0.079 [−0.146, −0.013]). Early openness is a between-concept trait, not a within-concept mechanism. Code: closure test

Vocabulary-free replication on 636 phrase-born concepts gives OPENhome PSP +0.117 [+0.020, +0.218] at control rung R3; the frozen verdict is PARTIAL (at the most demanding rung R5 the interval includes zero, +0.086 [−0.009, +0.190], and Holm p = 0.052). The signal is carried by neighbourhood novelty (NOVres +0.208 [+0.113, +0.303]). Code: vocabulary-free test

Limitations

  • Effect size. The OPEN–breadth association is modest. OPENhome adds no forecasting gain over B5 (+0.002 [−0.003, +0.008] Spearman on the fresh cohort).
  • Measurement noise. Split-half reliability of OPENhome is 0.49. The temporal-excess variants have reliability below 0.05, so temporal versus static dispersion cannot be resolved at available sample sizes.
  • Vocabulary bias. The legacy OpenAlex concept vocabulary is survivor-selected. The vocabulary-free frame is small (636 concepts) with pre-unseal power of 0.47.
  • Coupling. About half of the OPENall signal is mechanical coupling between the ego-network construction (which uses off-home papers) and the breadth outcome. OPENhome is free of this coupling but marginal.
  • Domain heterogeneity. The home-only OPEN signal is weakest in Physical Sciences (PSP +0.025); in the all-papers build Life & Environment is weakest (+0.071). The retained-field relatedness I² is 0.92.
  • Not causal. Early ego-network openness could reflect a concept's intrinsic generality, the diversity of the research community around it, or the breadth of the problems it addresses.
  • Prior unsealing. Held-out outcomes were previously unsealed by two earlier experiments in the same project, so the indicator-screen held-out results are robustness checks, not fully independent confirmations.
