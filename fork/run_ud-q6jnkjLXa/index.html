Early co-occurrence network openness predicts cross-disciplinary concept breadth: evidence from 12,499 scientific concepts

AI Inventor

What determines whether a new scientific concept spreads across disciplines or stays confined to its home field? This study tracked 12,499 concepts from the OpenAlex bibliometric corpus and found that concepts whose early co-occurrence neighbours span diverse topic communities tend to reach more fields later. The pooled partial correlation between this early network openness and later cross-field breadth is +0.069, small but consistently positive across all six independent validation bodies. A separate field-entry model shows that concepts spread next to fields related to those currently retaining them, though the pre-registered verdict on that claim is partial.

Contributions

53 indicators screened, 7 confirmed on held-out data

53 early co-occurrence network indicators from six families were tested against a five-feature popularity baseline for predicting rarefied cross-field breadth. Seven survive held-out validation with Holm correction, spanning retained-frontier and ego-network topology families. Two confirmed indicators carry negative signs: early retention ratio and ego density.

The OPEN index: a composite of early neighbourhood openness

A six-component index measuring topical non-redundancy in a concept's early co-occurrence neighbourhood. Pooled across six non-selection bodies, the partial Spearman correlation of OPENhome with rarefied breadth is +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100], with I2 = 0 and consistent positive sign in every body.

Concepts spread next to fields that retain them

A conditional logit on an independent frame of 11,841 concepts shows that concepts enter fields related to those currently retaining them (d0 = 0.322 [0.291, 0.355]). The pre-registered verdict is partial: the volume-matched contrast is null, and the effect is specific to the PMI backbone.

Consistency predicts volume, not breadth

Rebuilding Cheng et al.'s ideational consistency on OpenAlex topic co-usage reproduces their volume prediction (+53.5%/SD) but reveals a reversal: net of volume, consistency predicts narrower cross-field reach (partial Spearman −0.069, I2 = 0, all five field groups negative).

Early contact diversity drives the breadth gap

Early contact diversity accounts for 73% of the breadth gap between broadly integrating and locally absorbed concepts. No discrete trajectory typology is stable; diffusion paths form a continuum along a breadth axis.

Method

Data and concepts

The study uses the OpenAlex bulk snapshot (476 million works, 129.4 million base works from 1995 to 2022). From 56,643 legacy OpenAlex concepts, Aho-Corasick title matching with stemmed verification plus a per-concept LLM precision gate (threshold 0.80) produces 12,499 grounded concepts. A concept's onset year t0 is the first year it reaches 20 grounded publications.

Panel design

Concepts are split into a development set (DEV: 4,771 concepts with homes in Computer Science, Engineering, Biochemistry/Genetics/Medicine and Medicine, onsets 2003-2009), four held-out domain groups (3,372 concepts in Physical Sciences, Life & Environment, Social Sciences, Math & Decision Sciences), a 2010-2014 onset cohort (4,356 concepts), and a fresh 2015-2017 cohort (1,443 concepts) used only for confirmation.

Outcome

The primary outcome is rarefied field breadth O2r (m = 50): the expected number of distinct venue fields among a fixed-size random draw of 50 papers from a concept's publications in years t0+6 to t0+8, computed by exact hypergeometric rarefaction. Rarefaction separates size-adjusted breadth from sheer volume.

Indicator screening

53 candidate indicators across six families (ego-network topology, popularity, disciplinary spread, retained frontier, gateway centrality, co-author reach) are computed over the early window t0 to t0+2 and tested against a five-feature popularity baseline (B5: log early volume, growth, non-home share, Shannon entropy, field reach). Screening uses partial Spearman priority with 2,000 concept-level bootstrap resamples. Pooling across field groups uses DerSimonian-Laird random-effects estimation. Confirmation requires Holm-corrected p < 0.05 and a 95% CI excluding zero.

The OPEN index

The OPEN index is the mean of six signed z-scored ego-network components from the early window: new-edge rate, number of Leiden communities reached, participation coefficient, and neighbourhood novelty residual (all positive sign), plus ego density and edge persistence (both negative sign). The z-score constants are frozen from DEV. Three builds are tested: OPENhome (home-field graph only), OPENall (corpus-wide), and OPENsizematch (corpus-wide, subsampled to home counts).

Field-entry model

A conditional logit (Breslow partial likelihood) on concept-year risk sets tests whether relatedness to the fields currently retaining a concept predicts which field it enters next. Retention means at least two grounded papers per year in each of the two most recent years. Relatedness is positive pointwise mutual information (PMI) on the field-level backbone. The model is specified on DEV and scored once on an independent held-out frame of 11,841 concepts (6,978 entry events in 6,076 informative strata).

Results

Seven indicators survive held-out validation

Of the 53 screened indicators, seven are confirmed on held-out field groups with Holm correction. [code] They span two families: retained frontier (frontier density, volume-weighted field density, contact reach, retention ratio) and co-occurrence topology (community count, neighbourhood novelty residual, ego density). No centrality or volume-only indicator survives. Two confirmed indicators carry negative signs: early retention ratio (−0.114, concepts with higher early field retention spread less broadly) and ego density (−0.102, concepts with denser co-occurrence neighbourhoods spread less).

Frontier density at period end has the highest pooled partial Spearman (+0.375), but it counts how many fields already carry the concept by t0+2, so much of its signal is a pre-onset cumulative footprint. The paper headlines the OPEN index instead, because it captures a qualitatively different signal: topical non-redundancy in the early home-field graph, free of the mechanical coupling between off-home papers and the breadth outcome.

The OPEN index: pooled result

Partial Spearman +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100] OPENhome with rarefied cross-field breadth (O2r, m = 50), pooled across six non-selection bodies. I2 = 0, 6/6 positive. The development-set estimate (+0.109) shows 1.58x shrinkage relative to the non-selection pool. [code]

The OPEN index was confirmed on a 2015-2017 onset cohort (1,443 concepts) never used in any prior screen. [code] OPENhome passes the primary concept-type rung (R2: PSP = +0.091, 95% CI [+0.013, +0.171]). The paired difference OPENall minus OPENhome at the footprint rung is +0.093 [+0.016, +0.169]; about half of this gap is paper count. OPENall is partly mechanically coupled to the outcome because early off-home papers enter both the ego network and the breadth measure.

A vocabulary-free replication on 636 phrase-born concepts [code] gives OPENhome PSP +0.117 [+0.020, +0.218] at control rung R3. The verdict is partial: the most demanding rung's confidence interval includes zero (+0.086 [−0.009, +0.190]), and edge persistence is null (−0.013). The signal is carried by neighbourhood novelty (NOVres +0.208 [+0.113, +0.303]).

Consistency predicts volume, not breadth

Partial Spearman −0.069 [−0.093, −0.047] Cheng et al.'s ideational consistency with rarefied breadth, net of the popularity baseline. I2 = 0, 5/5 field groups negative. Replicates on the 2015-2017 cohort (−0.111 [−0.197, −0.030]). [code]

In the original design (next-year volume, no current-size control), the model reproduces Cheng et al.'s estimate: b = 0.428 (+53.5%/SD). Adding current volume removes almost all of it: +1.3% [+0.5%, +2.1%], a residual-to-raw ratio of 0.021. The consistency measure has Spearman +0.77 with edge persistence; it is weighted persistence, confirming that a stable neighbourhood predicts growth but not breadth.

Where concepts spread: retained-frontier entry

d0 = 0.322 [0.291, 0.355] Retained-frontier coefficient (log-odds of entering a field per SD of relatedness to off-home fields currently retaining the concept), on the independent held-out frame. R3 vs R2 likelihood-ratio 325.8, p < 10−70. [code]

The result is positive in three of three evaluable held-out groups: Physical Sciences +0.148, Life & Environment +0.401, Social Sciences +0.297. Mathematics & Decision Sciences is null (+0.065 [−0.109, 0.239], 165 concepts). The pooled estimate is 0.243 [0.118, 0.368] with I2 = 0.92.

Pre-registered verdict: PARTIAL. The volume-matched contrast (comparing entry rates of retained versus entered-but-not-retained fields in the same volume cell) is null: −0.028 [−0.105, +0.046] on held-out data. The dose response is not monotone. Under minimum-conditional-probability proximity instead of PMI, d0 = −0.021 (p = 0.012), while RCA density becomes strong. Persistence is confounded with volume, and the result is backbone-specific.

Where the breadth gap comes from

73% from early contact diversity The breadth gap between the top and bottom O2r terciles decomposes as: early contact diversity 77.9%, frontier advance −4.6%, retention 26.8%. These shares are an accounting identity, not causal effects. [code]

No discrete trajectory typology passes the naming rule. DTW k-medoids and HMM-based clustering give an adjusted Rand index of 0.222. PCA reveals a continuum: PC1 (38.8% of variance) is a breadth axis. Early openness correlates with PC1 beyond B5 (held-out partial 0.120, I2 = 0) but not with PC2.

Is the signal real? Confound analysis

Four null models tested whether the openness signal is an artefact of small samples or dynamic churn. [code] A within-concept permutation null absorbs year-to-year churn: excess NOVCHURN pooled PSP = +0.008 [−0.02, +0.03], compared to raw NOVCHURN = +0.116 [+0.09, +0.14]. The R2 of raw persistence on the permutation-null mean is 0.66. What predicts breadth is a static property of the concept's home-topic partner mix, not demonstrably temporal turnover.

A within-concept closure test [code] is null on the development set and opposite-signed on held-out fields (OPENhome b = −0.079 [−0.146, −0.013]). Early openness is a between-concept trait, not a within-concept mechanism driving diffusion.

Limitations

  • Effect size. The OPEN-breadth association is modest. OPENhome adds no forecasting gain over B5 (+0.002 [−0.003, +0.008]).
  • Measurement noise. Split-half reliability of OPENhome is 0.49. The temporal-excess variants have split-half reliability below 0.05, so temporal versus static dispersion cannot be resolved at available sample sizes.
  • Vocabulary bias. The legacy OpenAlex concept vocabulary is survivor-selected. The vocabulary-free frame is small (636 concepts) with pre-unseal power of 0.47.
  • Coupling. About half of the OPENall signal is mechanical coupling between the ego-network construction (which uses off-home papers) and the breadth outcome. OPENhome is free of this coupling but marginal.
  • Domain heterogeneity. Life & Environment shows the weakest OPEN signal (PSP +0.042). The retained-field relatedness I2 is 0.92.
  • Not causal. Early ego-network openness could reflect a concept's intrinsic generality, the diversity of the research community around it, or the breadth of the problems it addresses.
  • Prior unsealing. Held-out outcomes were previously unsealed by two earlier experiments in the same project, so the indicator-screen held-out results are robustness checks rather than fully independent confirmations.
