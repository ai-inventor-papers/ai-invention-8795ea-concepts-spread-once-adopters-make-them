Early co-occurrence network openness predicts cross-disciplinary concept breadth

Evidence from 12,499 scientific concepts

AI Inventor

Can the shape of a scientific concept's early topic neighbourhood predict whether it will cross disciplinary boundaries? We tracked 12,499 concepts across 26 fields in the OpenAlex corpus, screened 53 early network indicators, and confirmed seven on held-out data. Concepts whose early co-occurrence partners span diverse communities tend to reach more fields later: the pooled partial correlation is +0.069, small but positive in every validation body. Concepts also spread to fields related to those currently retaining them, but persistence and volume remain confounded.

Contributions

Seven confirmed indicators

Of 53 early co-occurrence network indicators screened against a popularity baseline, seven survive held-out validation with Holm correction. They span retained-frontier and ego-network topology families. Two carry negative signs: denser neighbourhoods and higher early field retention both predict narrower spread.

The OPEN index

A six-component composite of early co-occurrence openness. Pooled across six non-selection bodies, its partial Spearman correlation with rarefied cross-field breadth is +0.069 [+0.038, +0.100], with I2 = 0 and all six bodies positive.

Retained-frontier field entry

Concepts spread next to fields related to those currently retaining them (d0 = +0.322 [+0.291, +0.355] on an independent frame of 11,841 concepts). The pre-registered verdict is PARTIAL: the volume-matched contrast is null and the coefficient reverses under an alternative proximity measure.

Consistency predicts volume, not breadth

Cheng et al.'s ideational consistency reproduces their volume finding (+53.5%/SD), but net of volume predicts narrower cross-field reach. The reversal holds in all five field groups.

Contact diversity drives the breadth gap

Early contact diversity accounts for 73% of the gap between broadly and narrowly spreading concepts. No discrete trajectory typology is stable; diffusion paths form a continuum along a breadth axis.

Method

Data and concept identification

The study draws on the OpenAlex bulk snapshot (476 million works, 129.4 million base works from 1995 to 2022). Concepts are identified by Aho-Corasick title matching against 56,643 legacy OpenAlex concepts (levels 2–5) plus Wikidata aliases, with stemmed verification. A per-concept LLM precision gate drops concepts with precision below 0.80, yielding 12,499 grounded concepts across 26 scientific fields.

Panel structure

The panel split assigns concepts to a development set (DEV: 4,771 concepts in Computer Science, Engineering, Biochemistry/Genetics/Medicine and Medicine homes, onset 2003–2009), four held-out domain groups (3,372 concepts in Physical Sciences, Life & Environment, Social Sciences and Math & Decision Sciences), a 2010–2014 onset cohort (4,356 concepts), and a fresh 2015–2017 cohort (1,443 concepts) from a separate scan. The specification was frozen on DEV data and hash-sealed before held-out scoring.

Outcome

The primary outcome is rarefied field breadth (O2r, m = 50): the expected number of distinct venue fields in a fixed-size random draw of 50 papers from a concept's publications in years t0+6 to t0+8, computed by exact hypergeometric rarefaction. Rarefaction separates size-adjusted breadth from sheer volume.

Indicators and the OPEN index

For each concept, 53 early indicators in six families are computed in the t0 to t0+2 window: ego-network topology (27), popularity (6), disciplinary spread (3), retained frontier (7), gateway centrality (7), and co-author reach (3). These are screened against a five-feature popularity baseline (B5) using partial Spearman priority with 2,000 concept-level bootstrap resamples. Pooling across field groups uses the DerSimonian-Laird random-effects estimator with I2 heterogeneity reporting.

The OPEN index is the mean of six signed z-scored ego-network components from the early window: new edge rate, community count, participation coefficient, and neighbourhood novelty residual (positive signs), plus ego density and edge persistence (negative signs). Three builds are tested: OPENhome (home-field graph only), OPENall (corpus-wide), and OPENsizematch (corpus-wide papers subsampled to home counts).

Field-entry model

A conditional logit on concept-year risk sets tests whether relatedness to the fields currently retaining a concept predicts which field it enters next. A field is “retaining” a concept when it has published at least two grounded papers on it in each of the two most recent years. Relatedness is measured by positive pointwise mutual information on the field-level backbone. The independent frame comprises 11,841 concepts with 6,978 entry events in 6,076 informative strata.

73%
Breadth gap from early contact diversity

On 3,188 DEV concepts, the breadth gap between the top and bottom O2r terciles decomposes as: early contact diversity 77.9%, frontier advance −4.6%, retention 26.8%. On the held-out pool, the contact share is 0.633 [0.537, 0.727]. No discrete trajectory typology passes the naming rule; PCA reveals a continuum with PC1 (38.8% of variance) as a breadth axis. Code

Vocabulary-free replication

To address survivorship bias, 636 vocabulary-free phrase-born concepts (2003–2015 onsets) were tested. OPENhome gives PSP +0.117 [+0.020, +0.218] at control rung R3 for O2r (m = 30). The verdict is partial: the most demanding rung includes zero (+0.086 [−0.009, +0.190]), and the signal is carried by neighbourhood novelty (NOVres +0.208 [+0.113, +0.303]). Code

Confound analysis

Four null models test whether the openness signal is an artefact (code). Subsampling to 10 home papers per year retains 68% of the association. A within-concept year-label permutation null absorbs the churn signal: excess NOVCHURN pooled PSP = +0.008 [−0.02, +0.03] versus raw +0.116 [+0.09, +0.14]. The R2 of raw persistence on the permutation-null mean is 0.66. What predicts breadth is a static property of the concept's home-topic partner mix, not temporal turnover.

A within-concept closure test (code) is null on the development set and opposite-signed on held-out (−0.079 [−0.146, −0.013]). Early openness is a between-concept trait, not a within-concept mechanism. Temporal turnover is untested rather than refuted.

Limitations

Effect size. The OPEN-breadth association is modest. OPENhome adds no forecasting gain over B5 (+0.002 Spearman on the fresh cohort).

Measurement noise. Split-half reliability of OPENhome is 0.49. The temporal-excess variants have split-half reliability below 0.05, so temporal versus static dispersion cannot be resolved at available sample sizes.

Vocabulary bias. The legacy OpenAlex concept vocabulary is survivor-selected. The vocabulary-free frame has only 636 concepts with pre-unseal power of 0.47.

Coupling. About half of the OPENall signal is mechanical coupling between the ego-network construction (which uses off-home papers) and the breadth outcome. OPENhome is free of this coupling but marginal.

Domain heterogeneity. Life & Environment Sciences show the weakest OPEN signal (PSP +0.042 vs pooled +0.069). The retained-field relatedness I2 is 0.92.

Not causal. Early ego-network openness could reflect a concept's intrinsic generality, the diversity of the research community around it, or the breadth of the problems it addresses.

Prior unsealing. Held-out outcomes were previously used by two earlier experiments, so the indicator-screen held-out results are robustness checks rather than fully independent confirmations.
