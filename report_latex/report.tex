\section{Run overview}
\label{sec:overview}

This report is the internal, chronological record of a five-iteration research run investigating whether early structural signals in a scientific concept's topic co-occurrence network predict its later cross-disciplinary breadth. The run used the OpenAlex bulk snapshot (2026-09-23; 476{,}196{,}327 works; 129.4 million base works 1995--2022) and identified 12{,}499 concepts by Aho--Corasick title matching of 56{,}643 legacy OpenAlex concepts (levels 2--5) plus Wikidata aliases, with stemmed verification and a per-concept LLM precision gate ($\geq 0.80$).

The run addressed two research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} Which early co-occurrence network indicators predict size-adjusted cross-field breadth beyond a five-feature popularity baseline?
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} Do concepts spread next to fields related to those currently retaining them, extending the principle of relatedness?
\end{itemize}

The primary outcome is rarefied field breadth $O_{2r}$ ($m = 50$): the expected number of distinct venue fields among a fixed-size random draw of $m$ papers from a concept's publications in years $t_0 + 6$ to $t_0 + 8$.

Twenty artifacts were commissioned across five iterations; sixteen completed and four failed. Each iteration's strategy, review and hypothesis update are recorded in the sections that follow.

\section{Iteration 1: Racing rival spread signals on one panel}
\label{sec:iter1}

\subsection{Why this iteration}

The run began with five rival mechanisms for why a new concept becomes broadly integrated: (1)~lineage naturalisation (background-adjusted $A^*_h$), (2)~social reach through unconnected author groups, (3)~structural diversity of new co-occurrence neighbours, (4)~frequency-free selectivity, and (5)~landing in gateway fields. The strategy was a wide screen: test all five on one frozen DEV panel (48 concepts, CS/Eng/BGM/Med homes, onset 2003--2009), with a shared five-feature baseline (B5: log early volume, publication growth, non-home share, Shannon entropy, field reach), a shared outcome set, and a pre-registered survival rule ($\Delta\rho \geq 0.10$ with CI $> 0$, $\geq 3/4$ left-out groups positive, split-half reliability $\geq 0.6$, $|\rho|$ with volume/growth $\leq 0.6$).

Five artifacts were commissioned. Two failed (Experiment~2, candidate S for co-author independent groups; and Dataset~1, the sealed held-out test set), both due to REPL stalls. The three that completed are described below.

Additional findings from Experiment~1:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $A^*_h$ shows within-field sign flips: the Spearman correlation with O2r is $+0.45$ in Medicine, $-0.18$ in CS, so the direction of association reverses across fields.
    \item M1 background homophily: $R^2$ of the raw lineage log-odds ratio on the background log-odds ratio is 0.66 (90\% CI [0.39, 0.83]). All 48 concepts have a positive background log-odds ratio; two-thirds of between-concept variance in raw lineage autonomy is general disciplinary homophily, not concept-specific integration.
    \item The REML variance decomposition gives $\tau_c = 0.29$, $\tau_{cj} = 0.65$; a PyMC NUTS check agrees (Spearman 0.9996 with REML).
    \item Reliability-vs-$n$: $r_{SB} = 0.72$ only above 60 off-home children; on those 11 concepts, $\Delta\rho = +0.118$ [0, 0.355], underpowered.
    \item None of 14 candidate and foil features beats B5 ($\rho_{B5} = 0.834$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Data deviation:} The shared OpenAlex credit pool was exhausted (139 own credits spent). Yearly counts came from OpenAlex S0 exactly; field labels and citation lineage came from free Semantic Scholar data using fractional s2-fos text-classifier fields.

\subsection{Failed artifacts}

\textbf{Experiment 2} (candidate S: co-author independent groups, testing whether the number of disconnected researcher groups adopting a concept predicts spread) failed due to a REPL stall. Candidate S was untested at this point; it was later scored in Experiment~8 (iteration~3; S\_comp, S\_comp\_n, S\_isolated\_share) and was not confirmed for any outcome.

\textbf{Dataset 1} (sealed held-out Frame-N test set and 500-pair grounding benchmark) also failed. This meant no held-out evaluation existed at the end of iteration~1. Building the held-out set became the top priority for iteration~2.

\subsection{Iteration 1 review and hypothesis update}

The review scored 3 (blocking). Major critiques included: (a)~the conclusion that B5's ceiling (0.77--0.83) explains all nulls contradicted Experiment~4's own baseline ($\rho_{B5} = 0.327$); (b)~median $A^*_h$ values were mislabelled (they were within-group Spearman correlations, not medians); (c)~two failed artifacts were missing from the record; (d)~the three experiments did not share outcomes (cross-experiment O2r agreement only $\rho$ 0.76--0.80).

The hypothesis update moved to ``Gateway fields keep new concepts and pass them on,'' deepening the gateway-retention lead ($+0.103$ AUC) while building the full-corpus held-out frame.

\section{Iteration 2: Do hub fields keep new ideas alive?}
\label{sec:iter2}

\subsection{Why this iteration}

Iteration~1's only live lead was the field-level gateway-retention signal ($+0.103$ AUC on 80 episodes). Iteration~2 was designed to: (a)~build the full-corpus held-out frame from a zero-credit S3 scan (12{,}499 concepts); (b)~test gateway retention on the held-out panel; (c)~test field-entry via retained-field relatedness; (d)~stress-test the gateway lead; and (e)~position the results against prior work.

Five artifacts were commissioned; all five completed.

\subsection{Experiment 5: Do hub fields keep new concepts? Held-out test}
% gen_art_experiment_5

\subsection{Iteration 2 review and hypothesis update}

Review score: 3 (blocking). Major critiques included: the gateway retention signal was declared dead but the record still carried iteration-1 claims as ``confirmed''; the ordering claim was mixed (57/87 non-tied $= 65.5\%$ but only 32.6\% of broad concepts); and H3's pooled CI [$-0.006$, $0.065$] included zero.

Hypothesis update: ``Concepts spread from fields that keep them.'' Move: deepen. The retained-field relatedness result ($d_0 = 0.30$) was the lead, but required an independent confirmation frame. The co-occurrence indicators from Experiment~3 were recognised as a promising parallel track.

\section{What the run learned overall}
\label{sec:overall}

\subsection{Confirmed results}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Seven indicators survive held-out validation} for predicting rarefied cross-field breadth: M0\_density\_end, D\_vol\_end, CONTACT\_REACH, $n_{\text{comm}}$ (W3), NOV (novel-partner share), RETENTION\_RATIO (negative), ego\_density (negative). They span two families: retained frontier (4) and co-occurrence ego-network (3).

    \item \textbf{The OPEN index} (six ego-network components) is associated with breadth, marginally on the confirmatory 2015--2017 cohort (R2 $+0.091$ [$+0.013$, $+0.171$]; R4/R5 and the DerSimonian--Laird interval include zero). The Eval4 descriptive pool of OPEN$_{\text{home}}$ over 6 non-selection bodies gives partial Spearman $+0.069$ [95\% CI $+0.038$, $+0.100$], $I^2 = 0$, 6/6 positive.

    \item \textbf{Retained-field relatedness predicts field entry (pre-registered verdict PARTIAL):} $d_0 = 0.322$ [0.291, 0.355] on the independent frame (11{,}841 concepts, 6{,}978 entry events). The volume-matched contrast is null and the effect reverses under minimum-conditional-probability proximity.

    \item \textbf{The consistency--breadth reversal:} Cheng et al.'s consistency predicts volume ($+53\%$/SD, but only $+1.3\%$ once current volume is controlled) and narrower breadth ($-0.079$ pooled, 5/5 groups negative, $I^2 = 0$). These are selection data; on the vocabulary-free Frame~N the breadth association is $-0.064$ [$-0.159$, $+0.031$], so the reversal is not confirmed there.

    \item \textbf{Breadth is 73\% early exploration, 27\% retention} (unstratified variant; an accounting identity, not a causal split, because breadth and contact share papers). Early contact diversity ($E_2$) accounts for 78\% of the tercile gap; frontier advance is negative.

    \item \textbf{The signal is topical non-redundancy, not temporal turnover:} the within-concept permutation null absorbs the year-to-year component. What predicts breadth is a static property of the home topic partner mix.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dead ends and negative results}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lineage naturalisation ($A^*_h$):} failed 3 of 4 pre-registered clauses (only the size check passed). Within-field sign flips. Iteration~1 dead end.

    \item \textbf{Gateway-field retention:} the $+0.103$ AUC lead from iteration~1 (80 episodes) was a false positive. On the held-out test (8{,}515 episodes of the 27{,}393-episode panel), $\Delta$AUC $= -0.00001$. The shuffled placebo's 95th percentile exceeded the original. Dead end (iteration~2).

    \item \textbf{Co-author independent groups} (candidate S): the iteration-1 artifact stalled, but the three S indicators were scored in Experiment~8 (iteration~3) and none was confirmed for any outcome (e.g.\ S\_comp\_n on O2r\_m50 $-0.029$ [$-0.239$, $+0.184$]).

    \item \textbf{Gateway centrality for breadth} ($G_{\text{btw}}$): Holm $p$ not confirmed; CI includes zero.

    \item \textbf{Within-concept closure test} (Experiment~11): NOT SUPPORTED. Density and OPEN had null or opposite-signed effects. The event study was null. Dead end (iteration~5).

    \item \textbf{Trajectory typology:} no discrete classification passed the naming rule (DTW vs HMM ARI $= 0.222$). Breadth is a continuum.

    \item \textbf{Temporal turnover:} V2 excess NOVCHURN is null ($+0.008$), but split-half reliability is below 0.05, so the question is untested rather than refuted.

    \item \textbf{O5 external recognition:} unrelated to any publication outcome.

    \item \textbf{O1 gateway gains:} all 8 G-variant O1 gains were label-coverage artefacts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What is still open}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The OPEN$_{\text{home}}$ effect is modest (PSP $+0.069$ pooled) with no practical forecasting gain over B5.
    \item Split-half reliability of OPEN$_{\text{home}}$ is 0.49; measurement noise is substantial.
    \item The temporal-turnover question (is it year-to-year churn or a static dispersion property?) is untested rather than settled, because V2 excess reliability is below 0.05.
    \item The volume-matched retained-field relatedness contrast is null; $d_0$ reverses under an alternative backbone. Persistence versus sustained volume as a predictor of field entry cannot be separated.
    \item In the all-papers build, Life \& Environment Sciences show the weakest OPEN signal (PSP $+0.071$ versus $+0.186$ for the other five units pooled); the reason is unexplained. In the home-only build the weakest held-out group is Physical Sciences ($+0.025$).
    \item The persistence-filtered RCA density rival ($D_{\text{rca,persist},k}$) remains untested as identified by Research~2.
    \item Frame~N is small ($n = 448$ with OPEN$_{\text{home}}$) with pre-unseal power of only 0.47.
    \item About half of M0\_density\_end and D\_vol\_end is pre-onset footprint, not purely early network dynamics.
\end{itemize}
